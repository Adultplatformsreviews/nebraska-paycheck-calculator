Nebraska W-4N Allowances: How to Fill Out Form W-4N

Choosing the right number of allowances so your state withholding is close to your real tax

Why Nebraska has its own form

In 2020 the IRS redesigned the federal W-4 and removed allowances. Nebraska did not follow.

Instead, it created Form W-4N, the Nebraska Employee's Withholding Allowance Certificate. If you only gave your employer a new federal W-4 and never filed a W-4N, Nebraska rules generally require the employer to withhold as if you were single with zero allowances, which often means slightly more state tax comes out than necessary.

What an allowance does

Each allowance subtracts a fixed amount from your annualized wages before the Circular EN withholding formula runs. In recent schedules, one allowance is worth roughly $2,400 to $2,500 of annual wages.

At a 3–4% marginal rate, each allowance lowers yearly Nebraska withholding by about $75 to $100.

AllowancesAnnual wages removedLess withheld per year
1about $2,400–$2,500about $75–$100
2about $4,800–$5,000about $150–$200
3about $7,200–$7,500about $225–$300

How many to claim

SituationTypical allowances
Single, one job, no dependents1
Married, one earner2 + one per dependent
Married, both workingSplit allowances; often 1 each
Two or more jobs0 on the second job
Want a bigger refund0 or extra withholding

These are starting points. The W-4N worksheet walks through personal, spouse, dependent, and deduction lines to get an exact number.

Additional withholding

Line 2 of the W-4N lets you add a flat dollar amount per pay period. This is useful for side income without withholding, such as freelance work, or if you owed Nebraska tax last year.

Even $10 per biweekly check adds $260 a year.

Exempt status

You can claim exempt from Nebraska withholding only if you had no Nebraska tax liability last year and expect none this year. Exempt status must be renewed every year by February 15.

Nonresidents working in Nebraska generally still owe Nebraska tax on Nebraska wages.

When to update

  • Marriage or divorce
  • A new child or dependent
  • Starting or leaving a second job
  • A large refund or balance due on your last 1040N

After changing your form, check the next pay stub. For how allowances interact with the state brackets, read Nebraska income tax brackets.

Frequently asked questions

Is the W-4N required?

It is not mandatory to submit one, but without it your employer withholds as single with zero allowances. Filing one lets you fine-tune Nebraska withholding.

Can I use different allowances than on my old W-4?

Yes. The W-4N stands on its own.

Your federal W-4 entries do not control Nebraska allowances.

How do I lower my Nebraska refund?

Add allowances on your W-4N, then check the next stub. Each extra allowance reduces annual withholding by roughly $75 to $100 at current rates.

Where do I get the form?

The Nebraska Department of Revenue publishes the current W-4N. Give the signed form to your employer, not to the state.

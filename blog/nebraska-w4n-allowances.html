Nebraska W-4N Allowances: How to Fill Out Form W-4N

Choosing the right number of allowances so your state withholding is close to your real tax

Why Nebraska has its own form

In 2020 the IRS redesigned the federal W-4 and removed allowances. Nebraska did not follow.

Instead, it created Form W-4N, the Nebraska Employee's Withholding Allowance Certificate.

If you only gave your employer a new federal W-4 and never filed a W-4N, Nebraska rules generally require the employer to withhold as if you were single with zero allowances. That often means slightly more state tax comes out than necessary.

What an allowance does

Each allowance subtracts a fixed amount from your annualized wages before the Circular EN withholding formula runs. In recent schedules, one allowance is worth roughly $2,400 to $2,500 of annual wages.

At a 3–4% marginal rate, each allowance lowers yearly Nebraska withholding by about $75 to $100.

AllowancesAnnual wages removedLess withheld per year
1about $2,400–$2,500about $75–$100
2about $4,800–$5,000about $150–$200
3about $7,200–$7,500about $225–$300

You can see the exact effect for your pay with Nebraska tax withholding. Change the allowance count and watch the per-check amount move.

How many to claim

SituationTypical allowances
Single, one job, no dependents1
Married, one earner2 + one per dependent
Married, both workingSplit allowances; often 1 each
Two or more jobs0 on the second job
Want a bigger refund0 or extra withholding

These are starting points. The W-4N worksheet walks through personal, spouse, dependent, and deduction lines to get an exact number.

Additional withholding

Line 2 of the W-4N lets you add a flat dollar amount per pay period. This is useful for side income without withholding, such as freelance work, or if you owed Nebraska tax last year.

Even $10 per biweekly check adds $260 a year.

Exempt status

You can claim exempt from Nebraska withholding only if you had no Nebraska tax liability last year and expect none this year. Exempt status must be renewed every year by February 15.

Nonresidents working in Nebraska generally still owe Nebraska tax on Nebraska wages.

When to update

  • Marriage or divorce
  • A new child or dependent
  • Starting or leaving a second job
  • A large refund or balance due on your last 1040N

After changing your form, check the next pay stub. For how allowances interact with the state brackets, read Nebraska income tax brackets.

Who has to fill one out

If you gave your employer a federal W-4 on or after January 1, 2020, Nebraska requires a Form W-4N as well. The federal form dropped allowances that year. Nebraska kept them, because the state return still uses personal exemption credits, and the withholding system still needs a count.

A federal W-4 signed before January 1, 2020 can stay in the file. The employer keeps using that older form for Nebraska, and the marital status and the allowance count have to match what you claimed federally. You do not get to be "married with four" on one form and "single with zero" on the other when the federal form is the pre-2020 version.

Skip the W-4N after a 2020-or-later federal W-4 and the employer withholds as if you are single with zero allowances, no matter what the federal form says and no matter whether you are married. That default is the expensive one for a lot of households. It is legal for the employer. It is often more tax than you meant to prepay.

What one allowance is worth in 2027

Nebraska Circular EN values one allowance at $2,510 for the year. The employer subtracts the per-check piece of that $2,510 from wages, then looks up what is left. It is not a tax credit of $2,510. At a withholding rate around 3% or 4%, the tax saved is a few percent of $2,510, on the order of $75 to $100 across a full year if you stay in that rate the whole way. On a single $60,000 biweekly check, this calculator shows about $3.80 to $4 less Nebraska tax per extra allowance, which is about $99 a year. That matches the "around $100" sketch at this wage.

The 2026 value was $2,440 for the year, $93.85 on a biweekly check. A pay date still in 2026 uses the older amount. Daily, quarterly, and semiannual payrolls have their own slices of the same $2,510. The Nebraska tax withholding applies the weekly through annual rows and will show the Nebraska line move when you change the count.

A starting count, not a rule

The worksheet on the form is the real instruction. The rows below are only a place to start before you read it. They are not a Department of Revenue table.

SituationA common starting count
Single, one job, no dependents1
Married, one earner2, plus one for each dependent
Married, both workingOften 1 at each job, not 2 at both
A second job0 on the smaller job is a common choice
You want a larger refund, or you have other incomeFewer allowances, or a dollar amount of extra withholding

Two earners who each claim the full household stack will be under-withheld. Each employer can only see one job. Zero on the second job, or a flat extra amount on either W-4N, is how people close the gap. The federal W-4 has its own two-jobs box. Checking it does not fill in the Nebraska form.

On a $60,000 biweekly check in 2027, single, the calculator's Nebraska line is $77.59 at zero allowances, $73.80 at one, $70.01 at two, and $66.21 at three. Married, same wage and same pay date, the line is $67.24, $63.56, $59.88, and $56.20. Use your wage, not this one, before you decide the count is "wrong" because a coworker's stub differs by four dollars.

Extra withholding and the exemption box

The form has a line for an additional dollar amount each paycheck. That amount is added after the table. It does not change how many allowances you claimed, and it does not move you into a different bracket. It is the right tool when you have freelance income, a big second job, or you simply want the refund.

Exemption from withholding is narrower. You have to have had no Nebraska income-tax liability last year and expect none this year. The claim expires. A new W-4N is due by February 15 if you want the exemption again, and it is good for one year. Employers with more than 24 employees also face a special rule that can require at least 1.5% of taxable wages unless you document a lower amount. An exemption claim can lose to that rule. If the IRS requires federal withholding, Nebraska withholding follows.

Do not check exempt because you would prefer a larger check in March. If you owed Nebraska tax last April, you do not meet the test.

More than ten allowances

The wage-bracket grids in Circular EN stop at ten allowances. Above that, the employer subtracts the value of the allowances past ten from wages, then reads the ten-allowance column. The percentage method does not need the workaround. It subtracts every allowance first, then applies the row. Either way, allowance number eleven is not ignored. It is just off the printed grid.

Very high counts are unusual. They show up in large households or when someone is trying to zero out withholding without meeting the exempt test. The exempt box is the honest path if you truly expect no liability. A pile of allowances that does not match the worksheet is how people end up owing in April with a penalty conversation attached.

Form 9N is a different piece of paper

Form 9N is the nonresident certificate. It is for someone who works in Nebraska and in other states, and it tells the employer what percentage of wages to run through Nebraska withholding. The employer computes the tax on the full wage, then multiplies by that percentage. A nonresident who works in Nebraska 100% of the time does not use Form 9N.

The percentage withholds tax. It does not decide the Nebraska wages reported on the W-2. Those can still be the full Nebraska amount even when withholding was only on a fraction. A nonresident spouse of a servicemember who claims the servicemember's other home state uses Form 9N so the employer does not withhold Nebraska tax on the spouse's wages.

For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025, there are also narrow situations where withholding is not required on some nonresident wages, including seven or fewer duty days and certain convenience-rule pay. The conditions are specific. This site's calculator does not apply them. Put only the wages you believe are subject to Nebraska withholding into the tool, or leave Form 9N's percentage as a manual step after you see the full-wage tax.

When to hand in a new form

Marriage, divorce, a new dependent, a second job, a spouse going back to work, or a spouse leaving a job are the ordinary reasons. A raise by itself does not require a new W-4N. The tables already apply higher rows to higher wages. What the raise does not fix is a count that was wrong before the raise.

Give the form to the employer, not to the Department of Revenue. It stays in the payroll file. After the next check, compare the Nebraska line with the Nebraska tax withholding using the same gross, the same frequency, the same allowance count, and a 2027 pay date if the check is in 2027. A difference of a dollar or two can be the wage-bracket grid versus the percentage method. A difference of a whole allowance means the payroll file and the form you think you submitted are not the same form.

The brackets those allowances are offsetting are described on the Nebraska income tax brackets. The short version of "what percent is this?" is the Nebraska state tax rate. Neither one tells you how many allowances to write down. The worksheet on the W-4N does.

A ten-minute check after the form is in

Write down the count you submitted and the extra dollar amount, if any. When the next stub arrives, divide the gross by the number of checks that gross represents for the year only if you are comparing it with an annual example. Otherwise stay on one check. Put that gross, the pay frequency, the allowance count, and a 2027 pay date into the withholding calculator.

The Nebraska figures should be close. Wage-bracket employers can differ by a dollar or two from the percentage method. A gap the size of one allowance — about $4 on the $60,000 biweekly example, more or less at other wages — means the count in the payroll file is probably not the count on your copy. A gap the size of the whole state tax means the form never made it into the system and you are still on single with zero, or an exemption expired on February 15 and nobody told you.

Keep the copy. The employer keeps the original. Next February, if you claimed exempt, the copy is also your reminder that the claim is already dead unless you sign a new one.

Frequently asked questions

Is the W-4N required?

It is not mandatory to submit one, but without it your employer withholds as single with zero allowances. Filing one lets you fine-tune Nebraska withholding.

Can I use different allowances than on my old W-4?

Yes. The W-4N stands on its own.

Your federal W-4 entries do not control Nebraska allowances.

How do I lower my Nebraska refund?

Add allowances on your W-4N, then check the next stub. Each extra allowance reduces annual withholding by roughly $75 to $100 at current rates.

Where do I get the form?

The Nebraska Department of Revenue publishes the current W-4N. Give the signed form to your employer, not to the state.

Nebraska Minimum Wage 2027: $15.26 an Hour

The $15 hourly floor, who it covers, and what it looks like after taxes

The 2027 rate

On January 1, 2027, Nebraska's minimum wage is $15.26 an hour. The rate for 2026 was $15.00. The Department of Labor's minimum-wage schedule, updated July 31, 2026, is the source for the figures in this guide.

The same schedule already lists the next several January 1 rates, so "what will next year be?" is not a guess.

Voters passed Initiative 433 in 2022. That measure stepped the wage from $9.00 to $10.50 in 2023, $12.00 in 2024, $13.50 in 2025, and $15.00 in 2026. The later dollar amounts in the table are the ones the Department of Labor has published, not a fresh CPI estimate made on this page.

The youth rate for 14- and 15-year-olds is $13.50 beginning July 18, 2026, holds at $13.50 through 2029, and becomes $13.70 on January 1, 2030. After that, the schedule increases the youth rate by 1.5% every fifth year.

Who has to pay it

The state minimum applies to employers with four or more employees at any one time. Federal minimum wage is still $7.25. When the two differ, the worker gets the higher one, which in Nebraska is the state rate.

Some jobs are exempt under the statute's exemption list. The poster also allows a training wage for a new employee who is 16, 17, 18, or 19, is not a seasonal or migrant worker, for the first 90 days of employment, on the terms in that section. This guide does not print a training-wage dollar amount, because the poster points to the statute for the rate rather than repeating a second hourly figure. Student learners in a bona fide vocational program must be paid at least 75% of the applicable minimum.

Tipped employees can be paid a cash wage of $2.13 an hour. Tips plus the cash wage have to reach the full minimum that applies to that worker. If a slow shift does not get there, the employer makes up the difference. A $2.13 cash wage is not the minimum by itself.

The schedule is statewide. This guide uses the same $15.26 for Omaha, Lincoln, Grand Island, and a town with one grocery store. It does not apply a separate city minimum.

Gross pay at $15.26

Multiply the rate by the hours you actually work. A 40-hour week is $610.40. Fifty-two of those weeks, with no overtime, is $31,740.80 for the year. That is the gross, the number before federal tax, FICA, and Nebraska tax.

What is left after taxes

Gross is not the deposit. FICA takes 7.65% from the first dollar: 6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare. Federal income tax and Nebraska income tax depend on the W-4, the W-4N, and how large the annualized wage is. At this pay level both income taxes are modest. FICA is the line you cannot miss.

Benefits change the picture faster than the minimum wage does. A pre-tax health premium reduces wages before tax. A traditional 401(k) reduces income tax and does not reduce FICA. If your stub does not match the table, those deductions are the first place to look, along with a different allowance count. The hourly paycheck calculator is where you enter your own hours and rate. Overtime at the minimum

Nebraska follows the federal overtime rule for non-exempt workers: 1.5 times the regular rate after 40 hours in the workweek. At $15.26, time-and-a-half is $22.89.

Five overtime hours are $114.45 of extra gross. A week of 40 regular hours plus those five is $724.85 gross. On the same 2027 assumptions as the table, the calculator estimates $45.06 federal, $55.45 FICA, $20.19 Nebraska, and $604.15 net. The extra gross was $114.45. The extra net, compared with the straight $516.48 week, is about $88. The rest is tax.

The overtime rate uses the regular rate for that week. A shift differential can change the regular rate. This page does not blend those rates. Put the rate you were paid into the hourly calculator if you want a deposit estimate. Withholding on other overtime wages is in Nebraska overtime pay.

Breaks, paydays, and the stub

Nebraska does not have a general state law that requires meal or rest breaks for adults. A few industries have their own rules. A missing break and a short paycheck are different problems.

On the first check of 2027, the hourly rate on the stub should be at least $15.26 if you are a covered worker who is not in one of the limited lower-rate categories. Hours should match what you worked, including overtime at $22.89 or higher if your regular rate was higher. Tips, if you have them, should be enough that cash wage plus tips reach $15.26. If they do not, the makeup pay belongs on the stub.

Keep your own hours. A phone note or a copy of the schedule is enough when a check is short. Wage claims go to the Nebraska Department of Labor. This page cannot file one for you.

Salaried jobs and the hourly floor

A salary does not erase the minimum wage for a worker who is non-exempt. The pay for the hours actually worked still has to average at least $15.26 in 2027. Divide the weekly salary by the hours worked if the schedule runs long. If the result is under $15.26, the hourly floor is the issue, not the job title.

Exempt salaried employees are measured against the federal salary tests, not against an hourly minimum printed here. Those federal dollar tests change on the IRS and Labor Department calendars. This guide does not substitute a figure for them.

Twenty-six cents, across a year

The step from $15.00 to $15.26 is $0.26 an hour. On a 40-hour week that is $10.40 more gross. Over 52 weeks of 40 hours it is $540.80 more gross for the year: $31,740.80 instead of $31,200.

Tax takes a piece of the $540.80. It does not take all of it. FICA alone is 7.65%, about $41 of that annual increase, before federal and Nebraska income tax. The hourly calculator is the place to see the net on your actual hours. The point of the arithmetic is smaller: a 26-cent raise is a few hundred dollars of gross, not a new lifestyle, and it is still the legal floor for covered workers.

A tipped shift, in dollars

Cash wage of $2.13 against a $15.26 minimum leaves $13.13 an hour that tips have to cover. On a five-hour shift the cash wages are $10.65. The minimum for those five hours is $76.30. Tips have to make up $65.65 or the employer adds the difference.

A strong dinner can clear that easily. A slow Tuesday can miss it. The makeup is wages, and wages are subject to withholding like other wages. The $2.13 figure is not a tip credit you get to keep if the tips were short. Count the whole shift, not the best hour.

The youth rate changes the target. A 15-year-old at $13.50 needs tips plus cash wage to reach $13.50, not $15.26. A 16-year-old on the regular minimum is measured against $15.26 unless a training wage actually applies. Do not mix the two floors on the same timesheet.

Ages 14 and 15

The youth minimum is $13.50 an hour from July 18, 2026, and it is still $13.50 on January 1, 2027. Forty hours at $13.50 is $540 a week and $28,080 over 52 weeks. That gross is the ceiling only if the employer is allowed to use the youth rate. A 14- or 15-year-old cannot be paid the youth rate by an employer who is outside the rule, and an adult cannot be paid the youth rate at all.

In 2030 the youth rate on the same schedule becomes $13.70, while the standard rate becomes $16.08. Until then the youth figure holds at $13.50 even as the standard rate steps to $15.26, $15.53, and $15.80. The gap widens on purpose. It is not an invitation to pay a 17-year-old $13.50.

What to look at on the first 2027 check

Find the rate before you find the tax. If the stub says $15.00 in a pay period that falls entirely in 2027, and you are a covered worker on the regular minimum, the wage is short by $0.26 an hour. A period that straddles January 1 can legitimately mix $15.00 hours and $15.26 hours. Ask which hours were which before you call it a mistake.

Then check hours, then overtime at $22.89 if you crossed 40 in the workweek, then the tax lines. Federal, FICA, and Nebraska tax on a full-time minimum-wage check are real, and the weekly example earlier on this page puts the net near $516 under a specific set of assumptions. A net of $400 with the gross still at $610 usually means a benefit deduction or a garnishment, not a wrong minimum wage.

Employers of fewer than four people can fall outside the state minimum and still be inside the federal $7.25. That gap is large. If you are unsure whether the employer has four employees, the state rate is the one to ask about, and the Department of Labor is the agency that takes the question. This page is the schedule and the after-tax illustration, not a ruling on a particular workplace.

Frequently asked questions

Can an employer pay less than $15.26 in 2027?

Sometimes. Tipped workers may receive a $2.13 cash wage if tips bring them to the full minimum. Ages 14 and 15 may be paid the $13.50 youth rate. A training wage can apply to some new employees ages 16 through 19 for the first 90 days, on the terms in state law. Student learners in a qualifying vocational program must get at least 75% of the applicable minimum. Other covered workers at employers with four or more employees get at least $15.26.

Does $15.26 apply in Omaha and Lincoln?

The state schedule is statewide, and this guide uses $15.26 for both cities. It does not apply a separate Omaha or Lincoln minimum.

What are the rates after 2027?

The Department of Labor schedule lists $15.53 on January 1, 2028, $15.80 on January 1, 2029, and $16.08 on January 1, 2030. The youth rate stays $13.50 through 2029 and is $13.70 in 2030.

Do salaried workers get the minimum wage?

Non-exempt salaried workers still have to average at least $15.26 for each hour worked in 2027. Exempt salaried workers are covered by federal salary tests instead of this hourly rate.

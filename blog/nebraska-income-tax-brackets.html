Nebraska Income Tax Brackets 2027 by Filing Status

How the state's progressive rates work and what changed with the recent tax cuts

Overview

Nebraska taxes wages with a progressive income tax.

That means your income is split into slices and each slice is taxed at its own rate. Moving into a higher bracket only raises the tax on the dollars inside that bracket, never on all of your income.

For 2027 the statutory structure has three effective tiers after the phase-down passed in 2023 (LB 754): roughly 2.46%, 3.51%, and a top rate of 3.99%.

The top rate has fallen fast: 6.64% for 2022, 5.84% for 2023, 5.20% for 2024, 4.55% for 2025, and 3.99% for 2026.

Bracket thresholds are indexed for inflation each year. Check the Nebraska Department of Revenue's current tables for the exact dollar cut-offs for single, married filing jointly, and head of household filers.

How a bracket calculation works

Suppose a single filer has Nebraska taxable income of $50,000. The first slice is taxed at the lowest rate, the next slice at the middle rate, and only the portion above the top threshold is taxed at 3.99%.

The result is an effective rate well below the top marginal rate. That is why the headline top rate overstates what most households pay.

Nebraska taxable income starts from federal adjusted gross income, then applies Nebraska adjustments and the standard deduction.

Social Security benefits are fully exempt from Nebraska tax starting in 2024, and a portion of military retirement is exempt. That mainly matters for retirees rather than wage earners.

Brackets vs. paycheck withholding

Your employer does not use the filing brackets directly. Payroll uses Nebraska Circular EN, a withholding schedule built around the W-4N allowance system.

Those withholding tables use their own rate steps, which is why the amount withheld each pay period can differ slightly from your final liability on Form 1040N. Any gap shows up as a refund or a balance due in April.

To see how state withholding is calculated from your gross pay and W-4N allowances, use Nebraska tax withholding.

To see the full picture of federal tax, FICA, and Nebraska tax together, use the Nebraska paycheck calculator.

Is Nebraska a high-tax state?

Historically Nebraska sat in the upper-middle range for income taxes. With a 3.99% top rate in 2027, it is now below many neighbors and far below states such as Iowa's former schedule.

Nebraska also has no local wage tax: Omaha, Lincoln, and other cities do not withhold a city income tax, so the state rate is the whole state-level story for paychecks.

Property taxes, which Nebraska relies on heavily, are a separate matter and do not appear on a paycheck.

Key takeaways

  • Three tiers in 2027, top rate 3.99%.
  • Rates apply only to income inside each bracket.
  • Thresholds are inflation-indexed; confirm annually.
  • Withholding follows Circular EN, not the 1040N brackets.
  • No city income tax anywhere in Nebraska.

What "in a higher bracket" actually does

A raise does not pull your whole paycheck into the top rate. Nebraska splits taxable income into bands. The lowest band is taxed at the lowest rate, the next band at the middle rate, and only the dollars past the top threshold are taxed at 3.99% in 2027.

That is why two coworkers can both "be in" the top bracket and still pay very different shares of income in state tax. One just crossed the line. The other has a large slice sitting above it. The first dollar each of them earned is still in the bottom band.

The page above already lists the 2027 tiers the return uses: about 2.46%, then about 3.51%, then 3.99%. The Department of Revenue indexes the dollar cutoffs. This guide does not print a cutoff we have not pinned to this year's table, because a stale threshold is worse than none. The shape of the tax is the part that stays useful: three bands, and only the band you are in applies to the next dollar.

Marginal rate and the rate you actually pay

The marginal rate is the rate on the next dollar of taxable income. The effective rate is the tax divided by income. They are different numbers on purpose.

Someone whose last dollars are taxed at 3.99% still has earlier dollars taxed at about 2.46% and about 3.51%. Average them, after the Nebraska standard deduction and any adjustments, and the effective rate lands under the headline. Quoting 3.99% as "my Nebraska tax" overstates the bill for almost everyone who is not living entirely in the top band.

Withholding is a third number, and it is the one on the stub. On a $60,000 salary, single, one W-4N allowance, biweekly, no pre-tax deductions, and a 2027 pay date, this site's calculator withholds $73.80 of Nebraska tax per check. Across 26 checks that is $1,918.80, or 3.20% of gross wages. Call that a withholding share. It is not the effective rate on Form 1040N, because the return starts from adjusted gross income, not from gross wages, and it applies credits the paycheck never saw.

Filing brackets are not the withholding rows

Payroll does not look up 2.46%, 3.51%, and 3.99%. It uses Circular EN. For a paycheck dated in 2027, the percentage-method rows step through 2.26%, 3.22%, 3.69%, 3.81%, 3.93%, and 4.03%, after the allowance is subtracted. A 2026 pay date still uses rows that run from 2.26% up to 4.60%.

Both schedules can be right at the same time. One is the tax on the return. The other is the estimate the employer sends in during the year. The gap is why a refund or a balance due shows up in the spring even when every stub was calculated correctly.

How the top rate got to 3.99%

The cuts were a staircase, not a single jump. The top individual rate on this site's table is 6.64% for 2022, 5.84% for 2023, 5.20% for 2024, 4.55% for 2025, and 3.99% for 2026. The 2027 return keeps 3.99% as the top tier. The law that set the phase-down treated 3.99% as the end of that particular cut. A later bill could change it. Until one does, 3.99% is the top band.

Lower bands came down with the top rate, which is why the 2027 list is about 2.46% and 3.51% underneath 3.99%, not the older wider spread. The dollar thresholds still move with inflation even when the percentages hold still. A threshold from a 2024 article will not be the 2027 threshold.

Corporate rates were reduced on their own path. They do not appear on a wage stub, so this guide does not treat the corporate percentage as a paycheck fact.

Filing status changes the width, not the percentages

Single filers, married people filing jointly, and heads of household face the same three percentages. What changes is how much income fits in each band. Joint thresholds are wider, roughly twice the single thresholds in a typical year, so a couple is not pushed into 3.99% as fast as a single person with the same combined income. Head of household sits between those two widths.

"Roughly twice" is the pattern, not a promise that this year's joint cutoff is exactly double. Read the current table before you plan a wedding around a bracket.

Married people who file separate Nebraska returns do not get the joint widths. Two jobs in one household create a different problem, and it shows up in withholding more than in the bracket statute: each employer withholds as if it sees the only wage. The bracket math on the return sees both. That mismatch is fixed with the W-4 and the W-4N, which the Nebraska W-4N covers, not by pretending the brackets are flat.

Gross wages are not Nebraska taxable income

The brackets apply to Nebraska taxable income. For a resident, that figure starts from federal adjusted gross income, then Nebraska additions and subtractions, then a deduction. It is smaller than the number in your offer letter.

A traditional 401(k) deferral lowers the wages that show up in that chain. A Roth deferral does not. Section 125 health premiums lower taxable wages too. Social Security benefits are fully exempt from Nebraska income tax. A portion of military retirement can be excluded. None of those facts rewrite the three rates. They change how many dollars ever reach the bands.

Pre-tax deductions also change withholding during the year, which is why a stub from January can disagree with a stub from March after open enrollment. The bracket schedule did not move. The wage did.

Residents, part-year residents, and people who only work here

Residents are taxed on income from everywhere, with a credit in many cases for tax paid to another state. Part-year residents and nonresidents who have Nebraska wages file Form 1040N and use Schedule III to assign income to Nebraska. The same three rates apply to the Nebraska portion. You do not get a special "Omaha bracket" or a "remote worker bracket."

Cities do not layer a local income tax on top. Omaha, Lincoln, and the rest of the state share this schedule. Property tax is real in Nebraska and it is not on this table. It shows up on a county bill, not as a line that uses 2.46%.

If you moved in June, do not annualize a partial year's wages and then apply 3.99% to the result. The return splits the year. Withholding will not have done that split perfectly, because each employer only saw the checks it wrote.

A practical way to use the brackets

Use them when the question is about the return: a side business, a spouse's wages, a move, or whether a raise crosses into the top band. Use the withholding calculator when the question is about the check in your hand.

A workable habit is to look at last year's 1040N, note which band the last dollar fell in, and treat that percentage as the marginal rate on the next raise. Then run the new salary through the Nebraska paycheck calculator if you want the deposit, not the tax return. The deposit also loses federal income tax and FICA, which these brackets ignore.

If the marginal rate and the withholding share are far apart, the usual reasons are a W-4N that does not match the household, a second job, or income that never passes through payroll. Adding allowances to chase a smaller refund does not change the bracket. It changes the prepayment.

What this page will not pretend to know

Indexed dollar thresholds, the exact personal exemption credit, and any bill that has not landed in the tables yet are left out on purpose. The percentages above are the ones this site already uses for 2027. The withholding steps are the ones in the calculator's Circular EN tables.

When you file, the form instructions for that year win over a blog post. When you are staring at a stub, the withholding tool is the closer match. The brackets explain the bill. They do not compute the deposit.

Frequently asked questions

Does Nebraska have a flat tax?

No. Nebraska uses a progressive schedule with multiple brackets.

The phase-down narrowed the gap between rates, but income is still taxed in slices, and the lowest slice is taxed at roughly 2.46%.

Do married couples get wider brackets?

Yes. Married filing jointly thresholds are roughly double the single thresholds, so a two-income household is not pushed into the top tier as quickly.

Head of household filers have thresholds between the two.

Will rates fall further after 2026?

The 2023 law set 3.99% as the end point for the individual top rate. Future changes would require new legislation, so watch for announcements from the Legislature and the Department of Revenue each session.

Where do I report Nebraska income?

Residents file Form 1040N. Part-year residents and nonresidents with Nebraska wages file the same form and complete Schedule III to allocate income to Nebraska.

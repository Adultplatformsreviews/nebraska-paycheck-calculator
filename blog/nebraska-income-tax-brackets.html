Nebraska Income Tax Brackets for 2026 and 2027

By Nebraska Paycheck Calculator · Updated November 9, 2026

Filing-status thresholds, the 2026 and 2027 rates, and how the top rate phased down

2026 and 2027 rates

Nebraska taxes income in brackets. A higher bracket taxes only the dollars inside that bracket. It does not re-tax the income below it.

The 2026 return uses 2.46%, 3.51%, and a top rate of 4.55%. For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2027, the top two brackets fall to 3.99%. The first two rates stay 2.46% and 3.51%. In both years the third and fourth brackets share one percentage, so there are three effective rates.

If the question is the overall burden — top rate, sales tax, local sales tax, or the share of a paycheck — that answer is the Nebraska state tax rate. This page is the bracket table.

Joint thresholds are about twice the single thresholds. Head of household sits between them. A couple is not pushed into the top slice as fast as a single filer with the same combined income. Two jobs still withhold as if each employer sees the only wage. That gap is fixed on the Nebraska W-4N, not by widening a bracket on the stub.

2027 thresholds are not published here

The 2027 percentages are set: 2.46%, 3.51%, and 3.99% on the top two brackets. The dollar cutoffs are indexed for inflation and published by the Tax Commissioner. This page does not print a 2027 dollar threshold the Department has not released. The shape stays the same as 2026: four brackets, joint about twice single, head of household in between, and only the slice you are in applies to the next dollar.

A single filer with $50,000 of Nebraska taxable income in 2026 is over $39,900. The schedule is $1,514.58 plus 4.55% of the excess over $39,900. The excess is $10,100. That is $459.55, and the tax before credits is $1,974.13, about 3.95% of that taxable income. The 4.55% rate applied only above $24,760. Quoting the top rate as "the" rate overstates the bill.

How the top rate phased in

The cuts were a staircase. The top individual rate (the fourth bracket) was 6.84% for 2022, then 6.64% for 2023, 5.84% for 2024, 5.20% for 2025, and 4.55% for 2026. Tax years beginning in 2027 use 3.99%. The 2023 law treated 3.99% as the end of that phase-down. A later bill could change it. Until one does, 3.99% is the 2027 top band, and 4.55% is still the 2026 top band.

Lower brackets came down with the top rate, which is why 2026 is 2.46% and 3.51% under a 4.55% top, and 2027 is 2.46% and 3.51% under a 3.99% top. Corporate rates followed their own path and do not appear on a wage stub.

What the brackets apply to

The brackets apply to Nebraska taxable income, not to the salary in an offer letter. For a resident, that figure starts from federal adjusted gross income, then Nebraska additions and subtractions, then a deduction such as the Nebraska standard deduction.

A traditional 401(k) deferral lowers the wages that reach the brackets. A Roth deferral does not. Social Security benefits are exempt from Nebraska income tax. A portion of military retirement can be excluded. None of those facts change the rates. They change how many dollars reach the bands.

Residents are taxed on income from everywhere, with a credit in many cases for tax paid to another state. Part-year residents and nonresidents with Nebraska wages file Form 1040N and use Schedule III to assign income to Nebraska. The same brackets apply to the Nebraska portion. There is no Omaha bracket and no remote-worker bracket.

Brackets are not paycheck withholding

Payroll does not look up this table. It uses Nebraska Circular EN. A 2027 paycheck steps through withholding rows from about 2.26% to 4.03% after W-4N allowances. A 2026 paycheck still uses rows from about 2.26% to 4.60%. Both can be right at the same time: one schedule is the return, the other is the prepayment.

The worked percentage-method steps live on the Circular EN guide. The tool that runs them is Nebraska tax withholding. The year-end gap is Nebraska Form 1040N vs paycheck withholding. The full deposit, including federal tax and FICA, is the Nebraska paycheck calculator.

Key takeaways

  • 2026 brackets: 2.46%, 3.51%, and 4.55%, with published dollar thresholds for single, joint, and head of household.
  • 2027 keeps 2.46% and 3.51% and cuts the top two brackets to 3.99%. Dollar cutoffs for 2027 are indexed and are not printed here until they are published.
  • The top rate walked from 6.84% in 2022 to 4.55% in 2026 and 3.99% in 2027.
  • A higher bracket taxes only the slice inside it.
  • Withholding follows Circular EN, not this filing table. No Nebraska city adds a local wage tax.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Nebraska tax brackets for 2026?

Single filers and married people filing separately: 2.46% to $4,130, 3.51% to $24,760, and 4.55% above that, with the top bracket formally beginning over $39,900 at the same 4.55% rate. Joint filers: 2.46% to $8,250, 3.51% to $49,530, and 4.55% above that, with the top bracket over $79,800. Head of household: 2.46% to $7,700, 3.51% to $39,620, and 4.55% above that, with the top bracket over $59,160.

What are the Nebraska tax brackets for 2027?

The rates are 2.46%, 3.51%, and 3.99% on the top two brackets. The dollar thresholds are inflation-indexed and are published by the Tax Commissioner. This page does not guess those cutoffs.

Does Nebraska have a flat tax?

No. Income is still taxed in slices. The phase-down narrowed the rates, and in both 2026 and 2027 the third and fourth brackets happen to use the same percentage, but the lower slices stay at 2.46% and 3.51%.

Do married couples get wider brackets?

Yes. On the 2026 schedule, joint thresholds are about double the single thresholds. Head of household sits between the two. Married filing separately uses the single schedule.

Where do I report Nebraska income?

Residents file Form 1040N. Part-year residents and nonresidents with Nebraska wages file the same form and complete Schedule III to allocate income to Nebraska.

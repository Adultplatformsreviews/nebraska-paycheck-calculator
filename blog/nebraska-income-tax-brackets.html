2026 and 2027 Tax Brackets: Nebraska and the IRS

By Nebraska Paycheck Calculator · Updated November 18, 2026

Nebraska filing brackets for 2026 and 2027, and the 2027 IRS federal brackets from Revenue Procedure 2026-35.

2026 and 2027 rates

Nebraska taxes income in brackets. A higher bracket taxes only the dollars inside that bracket.

It does not re-tax the income below it.

The 2026 return uses 2.46%, 3.51%, and a top rate of 4.55%. For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2027, the top two brackets fall to 3.99%.

The first two rates stay 2.46% and 3.51%. In both years the third and fourth brackets share one percentage, so there are three effective rates.

2027 Nebraska brackets on the estimated-tax schedule

The 2027 rates are set in statute: 2.46%, 3.51%, and 3.99% on the top two brackets. Dollar cutoffs for estimating 2027 tax are in the Nebraska Department of Revenue's Form 1040N-ES booklet (Rev. 9-2026). The Department says to use that rate schedule only for computing 2027 estimated income tax, and not to compute an amount for a tax return.

The final 2027 Form 1040N calculation schedule is a separate publication. Until that schedule is the one in the instruction booklet, these are the official estimated thresholds, not a guess and not a filed-return table. The shape matches 2026: four rows, joint wider than single, head of household in between, and only the slice you are in applies to the next dollar. Married filing separately uses the single schedule.

How the top rate phased in

The cuts were a staircase. The top individual rate (the fourth bracket) was 6.84% for 2022, then 6.64% for 2023, 5.84% for 2024, 5.20% for 2025, and 4.55% for 2026.

Tax years beginning in 2027 use 3.99%. The 2023 law treated 3.99% as the end of that phase-down.

A later bill could change it. Until one does, 3.99% is the 2027 top band, and 4.55% is still the 2026 top band.

Lower brackets came down with the top rate, which is why 2026 is 2.46% and 3.51% under a 4.55% top, and 2027 is 2.46% and 3.51% under a 3.99% top. Corporate rates followed their own path and do not appear on a wage stub.

What the brackets apply to

The brackets apply to Nebraska taxable income, not to the salary in an offer letter. For a resident, that figure starts from federal adjusted gross income, then Nebraska additions and subtractions, then a deduction such as the Nebraska standard deduction.

A traditional 401(k) deferral lowers the wages that reach the brackets. A Roth deferral does not.

Social Security benefits are exempt from Nebraska income tax. A portion of military retirement can be excluded.

None of those facts change the rates. They change how many dollars reach the bands.

Residents are taxed on income from everywhere, with a credit in many cases for tax paid to another state. Part-year residents and nonresidents with Nebraska wages file Form 1040N and use Schedule III to assign income to Nebraska.

The same brackets apply to the Nebraska portion. There is no Omaha bracket and no remote-worker bracket.

Brackets are not paycheck withholding

Payroll does not look up this table. It uses Nebraska Circular EN.

A 2027 paycheck steps through withholding rows from about 2.26% to 4.03% after W-4N allowances. A 2026 paycheck still uses rows from about 2.26% to 4.60%.

Both can be right at the same time: one schedule is the return, the other is the prepayment.

The worked percentage-method steps live on the Circular EN guide. The tool that runs them is Nebraska tax withholding.

The year-end gap is Nebraska Form 1040N vs paycheck withholding. The full deposit, including federal tax and FICA, is the Nebraska paycheck calculator.

Key takeaways

  • 2026 brackets: 2.46%, 3.51%, and 4.55%, with published dollar thresholds for single, joint, and head of household.
  • 2027 keeps 2.46% and 3.51% and cuts the top two brackets to 3.99%. Dollar cutoffs for estimated tax are on Form 1040N-ES. Do not use that schedule to compute the final return.
  • The top rate walked from 6.84% in 2022 to 4.55% in 2026 and 3.99% in 2027.
  • A higher bracket taxes only the slice inside it.
  • Withholding follows Circular EN, not this filing table. No Nebraska city adds a local wage tax.

Single and $4,000 never leaves the first slice, so the whole amount is 2.46%, or $98.40. Single and $10,000 is $101.60 plus 3.51% of the $5,870 over $4,130, which is $307.64.

Single and $30,000 is $825.71 plus 4.55% of the $5,240 over $24,760, which is $1,064.13. The 4.55% rate touched only that top slice of the $30,000.

Single and $39,900 lands on the published base of the next row: $1,514.58. Single and $80,000 is that $1,514.58 plus 4.55% of the $40,100 over $39,900, or $3,339.13.

$3,339.13 is 4.17% of $80,000. The marginal rate on the last dollar is still 4.55%.

Quoting 4.55% as the tax on the whole $80,000 overstates the bill by using the top slice for every dollar.

Joint and $60,000 is $1,651.88 plus 4.55% of the $10,470 over $49,530, or $2,128.27. Head of household and $45,000 is $1,309.81 plus 4.55% of the $5,380 over $39,620, or $1,554.60.

Two boundaries, and only one of them changes the rate

On the 2026 single schedule, $1,000 that sits entirely between $4,130 and $24,760 is taxed at 3.51%, which is $35.10. The next $1,000, once taxable income has passed $24,760 and has not passed $39,900, is taxed at 4.55%, which is $45.50.

Same thousand dollars, $10.40 more tax, because the slice changed. The dollars under the boundary stay in their old slice.

Crossing $39,900 does not do that. The third and fourth single brackets are both 4.55% in 2026, so the next $1,000 after $39,900 is still $45.50.

The threshold is real on the form. The marginal rate does not step up when you cross it.

People who treat $39,900 as a cliff are reading a new row that happens to carry the same percentage.

In 2027 the top two brackets are both 3.99%, and the first two rates stay 2.46% and 3.51%. A $1,000 that is already inside the top band costs $39.90 of Nebraska tax instead of $45.50, which is $5.60 less on that thousand.

On the 2027 single estimated schedule, the 3.99% band starts over $25,570. Those dollar cutoffs are the Form 1040N-ES figures. They are not a guess, and they are not the final return schedule.

Uneven separate returns are not the joint row

Married filing separately uses the single schedule. Two spouses with $40,000 of taxable income each are each $100 into the top single slice.

Each tax before credits is $1,519.13, and together that is $3,038.26.

A joint return with $80,000 of taxable income is $200 into the top joint slice, and the tax before credits is also $3,038.26. At this particular split, separate and joint match before credits.

Move the same $80,000 so one spouse has $70,000 and the other has $10,000. The single-schedule taxes are $2,884.13 and $307.64, which add to $3,191.77.

That is $153.51 more than the joint tax on $80,000, before credits. The percentages did not change.

The dollars sat in higher slices because they were stacked on one schedule instead of spread across the wider joint thresholds.

Find the row without turning wages into the tax

Start from Nebraska taxable income on the return you are actually filing, not from box 1 of the W-2. For a 2027 estimate, use the Form 1040N-ES schedule above. The Department says not to use it to compute the return itself.

Find the row your taxable income has passed. Tax is the base on that row plus the row's rate times only the excess.

Then credits come off the tax. They are not a wider bracket.

A traditional deferral, an exempt Social Security benefit, or the Nebraska standard deduction changes how many dollars reach the row. None of them changes the percentages in the table.

Payroll will not show this arithmetic. A 2027 paycheck uses Nebraska Circular EN, and the year-end comparison is Nebraska Form 1040N vs paycheck withholding.

If the question is what percent of the paycheck was withheld, that is the Nebraska state tax rate page. If the question is the deposit after federal tax and FICA as well, use the Nebraska paycheck calculator.

2027 federal tax brackets (IRS)

People searching "2027 tax brackets," "2027 federal tax brackets," and "IRS tax brackets 2027" need the federal table, not only Nebraska's. The IRS published the 2027 inflation adjustments on November 5, 2026 in news release IR-2026-133. The full rate tables are in Revenue Procedure 2026-35. These brackets apply to taxable income on returns generally filed in 2028.

They are not paycheck withholding tables. The 2027 Publication 15-T is an early-release draft, and this site's calculator still withholds federal income tax with the 2026 Publication 15-T percentage-method worksheets. Nebraska withholding does switch to Circular EN 2027 on a 2027 pay date.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Nebraska tax brackets for 2026?

Single filers and married people filing separately: 2.46% to $4,130, 3.51% to $24,760, and 4.55% above that, with the top bracket formally beginning over $39,900 at the same 4.55% rate. Joint filers: 2.46% to $8,250, 3.51% to $49,530, and 4.55% above that, with the top bracket over $79,800.

Head of household: 2.46% to $7,700, 3.51% to $39,620, and 4.55% above that, with the top bracket over $59,160.

What are the Nebraska income tax brackets for 2027?

Statutory rates are 2.46%, 3.51%, and 3.99% on the top two brackets. Form 1040N-ES (Rev. 9-2026) prints estimated thresholds: single filers hit 3.51% over $4,270 and 3.99% over $25,570, with the top row formally beginning over $41,200 at the same 3.99%.

Joint filers: 2.46% to $8,520, 3.51% to $51,150, and 3.99% above that, with the top row over $82,410. The Department says this schedule is for estimated tax, not for computing the Form 1040N return.

What are the 2027 federal tax brackets and IRS tax brackets?

Revenue Procedure 2026-35, summarized in IR-2026-133, sets the 2027 ordinary rates at 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%. For a single filer the 22% bracket starts over $52,025 and the 37% bracket starts over $661,375.

Married filing jointly, 22% starts over $104,050 and 37% starts over $793,650. These are filing brackets. Paycheck withholding on this site still uses the 2026 Publication 15-T worksheets.

What are the Nebraska state income tax brackets?

Nebraska state income tax brackets for 2026 are 2.46%, 3.51%, and 4.55%, with published dollar thresholds by filing status. For 2027 the top two brackets are 3.99%, and the estimated dollar thresholds are the Form 1040N-ES figures on this page.

There is no separate Omaha bracket.

Does Nebraska have a flat tax?

No. Income is still taxed in slices.

The phase-down narrowed the rates, and in both 2026 and 2027 the third and fourth brackets happen to use the same percentage, but the lower slices stay at 2.46% and 3.51%.

Do married couples get wider brackets?

Yes. On the 2026 schedule, joint thresholds are about double the single thresholds.

Head of household sits between the two. Married filing separately uses the single schedule.

Where do I report Nebraska income?

Residents file Form 1040N. Part-year residents and nonresidents with Nebraska wages file the same form and complete Schedule III to allocate income to Nebraska.

What changed for a 2027 paycheck

Five figures are enough to date a stub. If a page on the internet still uses a different set, it is describing another year.

Federal income-tax withholding in this site's calculator still follows Publication 15-T for 2026, including on a 2027 pay date. The 2027 federal percentage-method tables were not the ones loaded in the tool. Nebraska withholding does switch with the pay date. A December 2026 check and a January 2027 check are allowed to differ.

The lower return rates under the top band are about 2.46% and 3.51%. They are not the withholding steps. Mixing those two lists is the most common way to misread a Nebraska stub.

Brackets, the rate, and the withholding form

Start with the bracket guide if someone told you that you are "in the 3.99% bracket" and you want to know whether that taxes your whole salary. It does not. Only the dollars past the top threshold use 3.99%. The guide also explains why the paycheck uses a different set of percentages. It does not walk the Circular EN worksheet. That worksheet is the withholding page's job.

The rate guide is the short version: 3.99% top rate, about 2.46% and 3.51% under it, 5.5% state sales tax, about 7% combined in Omaha and about 7.25% in Lincoln, and no local wage tax. It includes a table of how much of several salaries this calculator withholds for Nebraska, so "the rate" and "the share of my check" stop being the same sentence.

The W-4N guide is the form. One 2027 allowance is $2,510 a year, $96.54 on a biweekly check. You need the form if your federal W-4 is from 2020 or later. Without it, employers withhold as single with zero allowances. An exemption claim has to be renewed by February 15 and lasts one year. None of that is a bracket. It is the instruction the bracket math never sees until the payroll file has a number in it.

Wages and the deposit

The minimum-wage guide is the $15.26 rate for 2027, the published steps after that ($15.53, $15.80, $16.08), the $13.50 youth rate for ages 14 and 15, and the $2.13 tipped cash wage. It also shows what this calculator nets at 40, 25, and 20 hours. It is not an overtime treatise and it is not the hourly tool. If you have a rate that is not the minimum, leave that page and open the hourly calculator.

The take-home guide is gross versus net: which deduction hits FICA, which one hits only income tax, why a biweekly check and a semi-monthly check differ, and why a three-paycheck month is not a raise. The worked example is $60,000, single, one allowance, biweekly, 2027: $2,307.69 gross, $1,864.28 net. Married with two allowances on the same salary nets $1,962.04 in the same tool. Those are illustrations of the idea. Your benefits will move them.

The Omaha and Lincoln guide starts after that deposit. Withholding matches. Combined sales tax does not: about 7% in Omaha, about 7.25% in Lincoln. Council Bluffs residents who work in Omaha still deal with an Iowa return and a credit for Nebraska tax. The guide does not invent rents. It uses one calculated deposit, $1,717.31 biweekly on a $55,000 single salary, and tells you to set your actual rent next to it.

FICA and the employer's half

The FICA guide is 6.2% up to $192,300, 1.45% with no cap, and 0.9% Additional Medicare once one employer pays you over $200,000. Employee Social Security at the 2027 cap is $11,922.60. A traditional 401(k) does not reduce any of it. A Section 125 premium does. Two jobs can make you overpay Social Security, and that excess is a federal credit, not a Nebraska refund.

The payroll-tax guide turns the page around and looks at the employer. The match is another 7.65%. FUTA, after the usual state credit, is 0.6% of the first $7,000, or $42. Nebraska unemployment tax is the employer's and depends on the employer's rate. Form 941N is due at the end of the month after the quarter. Form W-3N is due January 31. Withholding payments have to be electronic if a prior year exceeded $5,000, with the booklet's example kicking in July 1, 2027 for employers who crossed that line in 2026. Employees do not pay those employer taxes, and the guide is written so a stub that shows unemployment as a deduction looks wrong, because it is.

A sane order if you are new to the state

Run the calculator first, salary or hourly, with a 2027 pay date. Note the three tax lines. Then read take-home pay if the order of those lines is the confusing part, FICA if the 7.65% is the surprising part, and the rate guide if someone quoted 3.99% and your stub does not show 3.99%.

File a W-4N if you have not. Read that guide before you pick a count, then come back to the withholding calculator and move the allowance field. If you are comparing Omaha and Lincoln, do the calculator once. Do not do it twice hoping for a city discount. There isn't one.

Employers who are costing a hire should read the payroll-tax page before they multiply $15.26 by 2,080 and stop. The wage is $31,740.80. The employer FICA match is about $2,428 more, and unemployment tax is still unpaid at that point.

What these guides leave alone

They do not prepare Form 1040N, they do not quote a bracket threshold that is not in front of us, and they do not set a training-wage dollar amount the labor poster does not print. Property-tax bills, inheritance tax, and corporate rates stay off the paycheck pages except where a guide says, plainly, that those taxes are not on the stub.

Figures that come from the calculator are labeled as this tool's estimates: single or married as stated, a 2027 pay date, and no pre-tax deductions unless a sentence says otherwise. Your employer's wage-bracket grid can differ by a dollar or two. A benefit you did not type in can differ by much more than that.

One number, followed through the set

$60,000, single, one W-4N allowance, biweekly, no pre-tax deductions, pay date in 2027. The calculator's check is $2,307.69 gross and $1,864.28 net. Federal withholding is $193.08. FICA is $176.54. Nebraska tax is $73.80.

The take-home guide uses that check to show the order of the lines. The FICA guide uses the $176.54 to show that 7.65% is larger than the state line. The rate guide divides $73.80 times 26 by $60,000 and gets 3.20% of gross withheld for Nebraska, which is not the 3.99% top rate. The bracket guide explains why those two percentages can both be correct. The W-4N guide moves the allowance count and shows the state line falling by about $4 a check. Same worker. Five different questions. None of the guides needs a second salary to make the point.

Change the worker to hourly at $15.26 and 40 hours and the minimum-wage guide and the hourly calculator are the two pages that should agree. Weekly gross is $610.40. The same assumptions net about $516.48. If a blog post and the calculator ever disagree on that pair of figures, trust the calculator and treat the post as stale.

What you type, on every calculator page

The salary page, the hourly page, and the withholding page share one engine. Pay date picks the Nebraska table: 2026 or 2027. Filing status and W-4N allowances pick the column and the subtraction. Pre-tax benefits reduce FICA and income tax. Pre-tax retirement reduces income tax only. Hours on the hourly page are hours per week, not hours per paycheck.

Leave a field blank and the tool assumes zero, which is a clean comparison and a bad picture of a job that has health insurance. Type the yearly benefit, not the per-check premium, in the benefits box. A $150 premium on a biweekly check is $3,900 a year, not $150 a year. That mistake will make the tax lines look mysteriously high.

Federal withholding stays on the 2026 Publication 15-T worksheets even when the pay date is in 2027. Nebraska withholding does not. If you are comparing this tool with a stub from January 2027, match the Nebraska line to the 2027 table and do not be surprised if a future federal booklet moves the federal line later.

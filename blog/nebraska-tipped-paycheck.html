Tip Withholding on a Nebraska Paycheck

By Nebraska Paycheck Calculator · Updated November 9, 2026

Reported tips, FICA, and the cash left after tax

Reported tips are wages on the stub

Tips a worker reports to the employer are wages for federal income tax, for Nebraska income tax, and for FICA. The employer withholds those taxes. Cash the guest left on the table can already be in the worker's pocket. The tax on that cash still has to come from somewhere, and the usual somewhere is the cash wage in the paycheck. The deposit gets smaller. The tips do not become tax-free because they skipped the direct deposit.

Charged tips often ride in the paycheck itself. They show up in the earnings block and they are paid on payday, so the gross and the deposit both include them. Cash tips often show up as "reported tips" or a similar memo. They increase the wage the tax is figured on, and they may not increase the deposit, because the worker already took the cash. Reading those labels is part of how to read a Nebraska pay stub. This page is the tax on the tips, not a tour of every stub column.

The cash wage an employer may pay before tips, and the makeup pay when tips miss the minimum, is the Nebraska minimum wage guide. This page does not restate that floor. It starts from tips that were reported and asks which lines on the check move.

A $400 cash wage and $800 of reported tips

Every tax figure below is this site's calculator for a 2027 pay date. The worker is single, claims one W-4N allowance, is paid biweekly, and has no pre-tax benefits. The tool has no separate tip box. Reported tips plus cash wages are entered as wages, which is how payroll treats tips the employer already has. Cash wages of $400 and reported tips of $800 are $1,200 of wages this check. Across 26 checks that is $31,200 for the year, so the row is a $31,200 salary.

FICA is 7.65% of the $1,200. Social Security is $74.40 and Medicare is $17.40. Nebraska withholding is $31.03. Federal withholding is $60.15. Federal dollars still use the 2026 Publication 15-T percentage method, which is what the tool uses until a 2027 federal edition is the one loaded. Nebraska dollars use the 2027 Circular EN table. One allowance removes $96.54 from the biweekly wage before that table runs.

If the employer pays the whole $1,200 on payday, because the tips were charged and are in the check, the deposit is $1,017.01. If the $800 was already received in cash, the same taxes still come out of the $400 cash wage. The taxes add to $182.98. The employer's deposit is $400 minus $182.98, or $217.02. The worker also has the $800. The value for the period is still about $1,017. The stub looks thin because the tips did not ride in the deposit. Both stories are the same withholding. They are not the same envelope of cash on Friday.

When the cash wage cannot cover the tax

The employer withholds the employee share of FICA on reported tips from the worker's cash wages. If this check's cash wages are too small, later cash wages in the year are the next place that withholding lands. What still cannot be collected does not disappear. The unpaid employee Social Security and Medicare remain the worker's, and they are settled with the federal return. They are not a Nebraska income-tax rate, and they are not a tip the employer gets to keep.

Federal income tax and Nebraska income tax that could not be taken from a small cash wage are also still part of the year's prepayment gap. The stub can be "right" and April can still want money, because the cash wage was never large enough to hold the tax. That gap is the same kind of gap as any other short withholding. The return side is Nebraska Form 1040N vs paycheck withholding. The FICA side, including tips as FICA wages, is FICA Social Security and Medicare.

Allocated tips are a different line. A large food or beverage employer may allocate tips when reported tips fall short of the employer's allocation method. Allocated tips are reported to the worker. The employer does not withhold on them. They still belong on the return. The examples on this page are reported tips the employer already has, which are the tips that change the stub. Do not subtract allocated tips from the deposit a second time. They were never in it.

The $20 month, and tips that never hit payroll

Tips of $20 or more in a calendar month are reported to the employer. The employer then withholds. A month under $20 does not have that payroll report. Those tips can still be taxable on the return. They will not appear on the stub, so the Friday deposit will not have withheld anything for them. A year of small cash months can look lightly taxed on the W-2 and still belong in income when you file.

Workers report the month's tips to the employer on the employer's timeline, commonly by the 10th of the next month. A tip that is reported late can show up on a later check. The year-to-date column is where you see whether the year's tips were actually added to Social Security wages. In 2027 the 6.2% stops once Social Security wages at that employer pass $192,300. Tips count toward that cap. Medicare does not stop. The cap itself is the FICA guide. This page only notes that reported tips are in the wage that the cap measures.

A weekly version of the same $31,200

The hourly tool can carry the same year if you put the tips inside the rate. Thirty hours at $20, with that $20 already including average tips, is $600 a week and $31,200 a year. FICA matches across the year because 7.65% does not care about the calendar. Nebraska does not match to the penny. Fifty-two weekly checks at $15.52 withhold $806.04. Twenty-six biweekly checks at $31.03 withhold $806.78. The year's difference is under a dollar. That penny gap is the per-period Circular EN table, explained in Nebraska Circular EN. It is not a special tip rate. People who compare a weekly stub to a biweekly friend's stub should compare the year, not one envelope.

The $20 rate in this row is an average that already includes tips. It is not a cash wage, and it is not a minimum wage. If the cash wage and the tips are separate on the stub, add them before you decide the calculator "missed" the tax. Entering only the cash wage will understate every tax line.

A federal tip deduction does not rewrite the stub

Reported tips are still wages for withholding. A federal deduction for qualified tips, in a year when one is available, is claimed when you file. It does not remove the 7.65% from the stub. It does not, by itself, change the Circular EN wage. Whether that deduction changes federal adjusted gross income, which is where Form 1040N starts, is a filing question for the year you are filing. Check the instructions for that year before you count on a smaller Friday deposit.

The hourly guide says the same thing in shorter form, because hourly workers ask it constantly. This page is the longer version for a check that actually contains tips. Overtime on a tipped job can use a regular rate that includes tips. The premium and the withholding on a heavy week are Nebraska overtime pay. This page leaves that math there. A bonus paid to a tipped worker is still a bonus. Nebraska bonus tax is the flat withholding method. Tips are not a bonus just because they vary by night.

How to use the tools without inventing a tip line

The hourly paycheck calculator is the right tool when you have a rate and hours. Include reported tips in the wage you type if you want the tax on those tips. The Nebraska paycheck calculator is the same engine with a yearly salary. A $31,200 salary, biweekly, is the $1,200 check in the table above. Neither screen has a box labeled tips. Payroll does not need one in order to withhold. It needs the tips to be in the wage.

Leave the rate as cash wages only when you are looking at the cash wage by itself. Say so, or the net will look high and the FICA line will look low next to a real stub that included Thursday's tips. Pre-tax benefits, if a tipped worker has them, come off before the tax. A cafeteria premium reduces FICA wages. A traditional 401(k) does not. Those fields work the same on a tipped check as on any other check. The benefits guide is HSA and health FSA on a Nebraska paycheck.

City does not change the tip math. Omaha and Lincoln use the same Nebraska tables. A local tax line on a tipped stub should still be zero. If a second state is withholding because the restaurant is across a state line, that is a different return problem. The border case is Nebraska vs Iowa paycheck.

Frequently asked questions

Are tips taxed on a Nebraska paycheck?

Reported tips are wages. The employer withholds federal income tax, Nebraska income tax, and FICA on them. Cash tips can make the deposit smaller because the tax comes out of the cash wage.

Does FICA apply to tips in Nebraska?

Yes. Reported tips are Social Security and Medicare wages. The employee share is withheld from cash wages. Uncollected employee FICA is still owed and is handled on the federal return.

Where is the $2.13 cash wage explained?

The cash-wage floor, the makeup pay when tips miss the minimum, and the youth rate are on the Nebraska minimum wage guide. This page does not restate that floor.

Will a federal tip deduction lower this week's Nebraska withholding?

No. A deduction for qualified tips, in a year when one is available, is claimed on the return. Reported tips stay in the wage that Circular EN and FICA use on the stub.

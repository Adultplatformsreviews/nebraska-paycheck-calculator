What Is the Nebraska State Tax Rate in 2027?

By Nebraska Paycheck Calculator · Updated November 9, 2026

The top income-tax rate, sales tax, local tax, and what share of a paycheck that actually is

The short answer

For 2027, Nebraska's top individual income-tax rate is 3.99%. The 2026 top rate is 4.55%.

The statewide sales tax is 5.5%. Omaha's combined sales tax is about 7%, and Lincoln's is about 7.25%.

Nebraska has no local income tax on wages.

It answers what percent Nebraska charges, and what that percent is not.

Marginal rate, effective rate, and what was withheld

The marginal rate is the statutory rate on the next dollar of Nebraska taxable income. For 2027 that is 2.46%, 3.51%, or 3.99%, depending on the band.

It is the right number for "how much of this raise does the state keep?" only after you know which band the raise falls in, and only for the state income-tax piece.

The effective rate is tax on the return divided by income. You do not know it for certain until the year is closed.

People often use last year's Form 1040N as a stand-in.

Withholding is a third number. Divide Nebraska tax on the stub by gross wages.

On the example this site uses often — $60,000, single, one W-4N allowance, biweekly, nothing pre-tax, pay date in 2027 — state withholding is $73.80 a check. That is $1,918.80 a year, and $1,918.80 divided by $60,000 is 3.20%.

That is not 3.99%, and it is not yet the effective rate on the return, because the return starts from adjusted gross income and applies credits the paycheck never saw.

Sales tax, property tax, and taxes that are not this rate

The statewide sales tax is 5.5%. Omaha's combined rate is about 7%, and Lincoln's is about 7.25% once local options are included.

Groceries are generally exempt. None of it is subtracted on a pay stub.

A 3.99% top income-tax rate does not price a cart of groceries, and a 7% sales tax does not price a raise.

Property tax is a third bill. Nebraska uses it heavily, and state credits can offset part of it.

It still does not show up as a percentage on the paycheck. Unemployment tax is paid by the employer.

The employee column is zero. Who pays which payroll tax is on Nebraska payroll taxes.

Local wage tax is the rate people ask about when they move here. It is zero.

No Nebraska city adds an income tax to the state rate. Omaha and Lincoln differ on sales tax and rent.

They do not differ on this percentage. Inheritance tax is collected at the county level and does not touch wages.

Federal income tax and FICA are real and large, and they are not the Nebraska state tax rate.

What changed, in one paragraph

The top individual rate stepped down over several years and is 4.55% for 2026 and 3.99% for tax years beginning in 2027. A 2023 stub and a 2027 stub at the same salary will usually show a smaller Nebraska line.

Part of that is the rate cut. Part may be a W-4N change, a benefit change, or the withholding tables.

The year-by-year rates and the 2026 filing thresholds are on Nebraska income tax brackets. A December 2026 check and a January 2027 check can also differ because they use different Circular EN booklets.

A raise, in one pass

Suppose the next dollar is in the top Nebraska band, the person is under the Social Security wage base, and federal withholding on that dollar is 22%. A rough keep-rate is 100% minus 22% federal, minus 7.65% FICA, minus 3.99% Nebraska, which is about 66%.

The sketch breaks if the dollar is in a lower band, if a traditional 401(k) or Section 125 benefits remove it from taxable wages, or if wages are already over the Social Security cap. Run the salary through the Nebraska paycheck calculator before you spend the raise.

A 2026 pay date and a 2027 pay date

Hold the wage still and change only the pay date. The same $60,000, single, one allowance, biweekly, with nothing pre-tax, produces two Nebraska lines.

A 2026 pay date withholds $81.70. A 2027 pay date withholds $73.80.

Federal withholding stays $193.08, and FICA stays $176.54.

The calculator's net is $1,856.37 on the 2026 date and $1,864.28 on the 2027 date. That gap is the rate change showing up as withholding.

It is not a raise, and it is not a new W-4N. The filing brackets behind the change are on Nebraska income tax brackets.

Do not average the two lines into one Nebraska rate. A December check and a January check can both be right.

Read the pay date on the stub before you compare it with a percent on this page.

Sales tax on a receipt, not on the stub

Statewide sales tax is 5.5% of a taxable purchase. On $80 of taxable goods that is $4.40.

Omaha's combined rate is about 7%, or about $5.60 on that $80. Lincoln's combined rate is about 7.25%, or about $5.80.

Groceries are generally exempt, so a grocery receipt is not this arithmetic. A restaurant meal usually is.

On a $250 taxable meal, about 7% in Omaha is $17.50 and about 7.25% in Lincoln is $18.13. None of those dollars is income-tax withholding.

Both get called "the Nebraska tax rate." One is a percent of a purchase. The other is a percent of income, and even that percent is not 3.99% of the paycheck.

City price differences, including these sales-tax gaps, are in the Omaha and Lincoln paycheck guide.

When the stub's percent is not the statute

Extra Nebraska withholding is a flat dollar amount added after the table. On the $60,000 biweekly example, an extra $25 moves the state line from $73.80 to $98.80.

$98.80 is about 4.28% of the $2,307.69 gross. That is above 3.99%, and the top rate did not go up.

Someone asked payroll to add $25.

A pre-tax benefit moves the base the other way. With $2,400 a year of pre-tax benefits on the same wage, this calculator withholds $70.17 of Nebraska tax, about 3.04% of gross.

The statute is unchanged. The wage the table sees is smaller.

Which benefits do that is HSA and health FSA on a Nebraska paycheck.

Marking exempt withholds $0 of Nebraska tax on this example, and the calculator's net is $1,938.08. Zero withheld is not a zero tax rate.

It is a claim about last year's liability and this year's. The rules for that box are on the W-4N guide, not a second rate.

Check the percent on one stub

Use the current-period Nebraska withholding, not the year-to-date column. Divide it by current gross pay.

On a single filer near $60,000 with one allowance, no pre-tax deductions, and a 2027 pay date, this calculator lands at 3.20%. A result near 0% is usually exempt status, a very small wage, or a state line that never got set up.

A result well above the shares in the tables, on an ordinary wage, is usually extra withholding or a bonus sitting in the same gross. A few tenths of a percent can be wage-bracket tables versus the percentage method.

Frequently asked questions

Is Nebraska's tax rate the same for everyone?

The schedule is the same, but the effective rate depends on income and filing status. Two people in the same city with different salaries pay different effective rates even though the top marginal rate is identical.

What percent of a paycheck is Nebraska state tax?

On this site's $60,000 single example for a 2027 pay date, about 3.20% of gross is withheld for Nebraska. Lower salaries in the table above land closer to 2.8%.

The top statutory rate, 3.99% in 2027, is not that percentage.

Do retirees pay Nebraska state income tax?

Social Security benefits are exempt from Nebraska income tax. Pensions, IRA withdrawals, and most other retirement income remain taxable, while part of military retirement can be excluded.

Does Nebraska tax remote workers?

Nebraska taxes residents on all income and nonresidents on Nebraska-source wages. If you live in Nebraska and work remotely for an out-of-state company, you generally owe Nebraska tax and should file a W-4N with that employer.

Is there an inheritance tax?

Yes. Nebraska is one of few states with a county-level inheritance tax, though close relatives receive a sizable exemption and pay a low rate.

It does not affect paychecks.

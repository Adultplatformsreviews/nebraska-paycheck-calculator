How a 401(k) Changes Nebraska Take-Home Pay

Traditional and Roth deferrals, and the 2027 tax lines that actually move

The deposit falls by less than a traditional deferral

A traditional 401(k) contribution is still your money. It leaves the paycheck and goes to the plan. Federal income tax and Nebraska income tax are figured on a smaller wage, so those two lines fall. Social Security and Medicare do not fall. The net deposit falls by the deferral minus the income tax that was not withheld.

A Roth 401(k) contribution is post-tax. The tax lines stay where they were. The deposit falls by the whole contribution. The account type does not change gross pay. It changes which taxes see the dollar.

The order of a normal check, from gross through post-tax items, is explained in Nebraska take-home pay. This page stays on the retirement line. The reason FICA ignores the deferral is in FICA Social Security and Medicare.

At 10%, federal withholding falls from $193.08 to $165.38. Nebraska falls from $73.80 to $64.73. FICA stays $176.54, because the deferred dollars are still FICA wages. Net falls from $1,864.28 to $1,670.27. The $230.77 that left for the plan is not a tax. Most of the smaller deposit is the contribution itself. Across 26 checks, the calculator's annual net goes from $48,471.18 with no deferral to $43,426.98 with the $6,000 traditional deferral. You did not lose $6,000 of spending money to tax. You moved $6,000 into the account and kept a smaller tax prepayment.

Roth on the same $6,000

Put the same $6,000 in as a Roth contribution and the calculator's post-tax field takes $230.77 from each biweekly check. Federal stays $193.08. FICA stays $176.54. Nebraska stays $73.80. Net is $1,633.51. Annual net is $42,471.18, which is the no-deferral year minus $6,000. The taxes did not share the cost.

A higher salary moves the federal line more

Married withholding gives back less per dollar

The take-home guide's married example is the same $60,000, biweekly, in 2027, with married filing status, two W-4N allowances, and no deferral: $109.23 federal, $176.54 FICA, $59.88 Nebraska, and net of $1,962.04. FICA does not change because someone is married.

Add a $6,000 traditional deferral under those same settings and the calculator withholds $83.85 federal and $51.22 Nebraska. FICA stays $176.54. Net is $1,765.32. The income-tax lines fall, and they fall by less than they did for the single filer with one allowance, because the married check was already withholding less. The deferral rule did not change. The starting tax did.

Two jobs in one household are a form problem. Each employer withholds from the W-4 and W-4N it received. This page does not retell how many allowances to claim. The Nebraska W-4N does.

The match is not a deduction

An employer match credited to the 401(k) does not reduce gross, tax, or net. If the stub shows the match, it is usually a memo of what the employer added to the account. Subtracting it again when you read the check double-counts a contribution you never paid from this deposit.

A match can vest on a schedule. Vesting changes what you keep if you leave. It does not change this Friday's net. A 401(k) loan repayment is different. Repayments are usually post-tax deductions. They shrink the deposit, they do not shrink Nebraska withholding, and they are not a new deferral. Read the label. "Loan" and "deferral" are not the same line.

Where it sits on the stub, and on a bonus

Traditional deferrals are pre-tax. Roth deferrals are post-tax. The current column shows this check's contribution. Year-to-date shows how much of the annual election has already been taken. Neither column is a tax. Labels and the year-to-date test are in how to read a Nebraska pay stub.

A deferral taken from a separate bonus, if the plan allows it, reduces the wages subject to the flat 22% federal supplemental rate and the flat 3.5% Nebraska rate. It still does not reduce FICA on that bonus. The bonus check itself is in Nebraska bonus tax. Do not assume every plan defers from bonuses. The plan document decides. The tax rule only describes what happens if the deferral is actually taken.

Which calculator field to use

The retirement field is a yearly traditional deferral. It lowers federal and Nebraska taxable wages and leaves FICA wages alone. The pre-tax benefits field is the other pile: medical premiums, a health FSA, an HSA through payroll. That pile can lower FICA too. Putting a 401(k) in the benefits field makes Social Security fall when a real stub will not.

Roth 401(k) contributions go in post-tax deductions, and that field is per paycheck, not per year. A $6,000 Roth on a biweekly calendar is $230.77 a check. Enter $6,000 there and you will have deducted $6,000 from every check.

The IRS sets a yearly cap on elective deferrals, including a higher catch-up for older workers. This page does not print a 2027 dollar cap. The withholding rule is the same on either side of the cap: traditional reduces income-tax wages, Roth does not, and neither reduces FICA. Enter the amount the plan will actually take. A percent change in March does not rewrite January's checks. It changes the checks after the payroll cutoff.

A larger traditional deferral moves the same three lines further and still leaves FICA alone. On this $60,000 biweekly example, $12,000 a year is $461.54 a check. The calculator then withholds $137.69 federal and $55.66 Nebraska. FICA stays $176.54. Net is $1,476.26. That is not a recommendation to defer 20% of pay. It is the same engine as the 6% and 10% rows, run at a bigger number, so you can see the income-tax lines keep falling and the Social Security line does not.

Health insurance is the deduction people mix up with this one. A pre-tax medical premium belongs in the benefits field because it can lower FICA as well as income tax. A 401(k) does not. If you are comparing an open-enrollment premium change with a retirement-percent change, run them as different fields. Combining them into one number hides which line actually moved.

Run a salary through the Nebraska paycheck calculator and edit the retirement field. Hourly pay, including a deferral, belongs on the hourly paycheck calculator. If you only want the Nebraska line, use Nebraska tax withholding. The year's prepayment versus the return is Nebraska Form 1040N vs paycheck withholding. A traditional deferral lowers both the checks and the wages the return starts from. It does not, by itself, create a refund.

Frequently asked questions

Does a traditional 401(k) lower Nebraska tax on the paycheck?

Yes. The deferral reduces Nebraska taxable wages, so Circular EN withholding falls. The return later uses that lower wage as well.

Does a 401(k) lower Social Security tax?

No. Traditional and Roth deferrals are still FICA wages. Social Security and Medicare stay at 6.2% and 1.45% of those wages until the Social Security wage base applies.

Why did take-home pay fall by less than my contribution?

The contribution left the check, and federal and Nebraska withholding fell. The difference stayed in the plan. The deposit only shows what remained after the deferral and the smaller tax.

Is a Roth 401(k) taken out before Nebraska tax?

No. A Roth deferral is taken after tax. Federal, FICA, and Nebraska withholding are figured as if that contribution were still in the wage. Only the deposit shrinks by the full amount.

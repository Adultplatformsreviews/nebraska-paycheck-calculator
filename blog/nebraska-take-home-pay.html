Nebraska Take-Home Pay Explained: Gross vs. Net Pay

Why net pay is smaller than your salary and which deductions make the difference

Gross pay vs. net pay

Gross pay is what you earn before anything is withheld: your salary divided by the number of pay periods, or your hours times your rate. Net pay, or take-home pay, is what reaches your bank account after taxes and deductions.

In Nebraska, most full-time workers keep roughly 75% to 82% of gross pay, depending on income, filing status, and benefits.

What comes out, in order

01
Pre-tax deductions

Traditional 401(k), health insurance premiums, HSA and FSA contributions lower taxable wages first.

02
FICA

6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare. 401(k) deferrals do not reduce FICA wages; Section 125 health premiums do.

03
Federal income tax

Based on your federal W-4 and IRS Publication 15-T.

04
Nebraska income tax

Based on Form W-4N allowances and Circular EN.

05
Post-tax deductions

Roth 401(k), garnishments, union dues, and similar items.

Why your take-home pay changes

Several things can change net pay even when your salary stays the same: open-enrollment premium changes, a new 401(k) percentage, or hitting the Social Security wage base late in the year (high earners see a raise when that tax stops).

A bonus taxed at the 22% federal supplemental rate, or updated W-4/W-4N forms, can change it as well. The state's annual rate cuts have also nudged Nebraska take-home pay upward each January since 2023.

No local tax helps

Unlike some states, Nebraska has no city or county income tax. Workers in Omaha, Lincoln, Grand Island, or Kearney all see the same state calculation.

Your location affects your cost of living, not your paycheck withholding.

Raising your take-home pay legally

  • Check that your W-4N allowances match your situation; too few means over-withholding.
  • Use pre-tax accounts like an HSA to lower taxable wages.
  • Review benefit elections you no longer need.
  • Remember: a large refund is money you could have had in each check.

Biweekly pay has two months a year with three checks. Those months feel like a bonus. They are the checks that make 26 instead of 24. Spending all three as if the raise is permanent is how the other ten months get tight. The annual math did not change.

Why the kept share shrinks as pay rises

FICA is a flat 7.65% until the Social Security wage base. Federal and Nebraska tax are not flat. Higher wages sit in higher bands, so the slice that reaches the bank gets smaller even though nobody took "a bigger percentage of everything."

Under the same single, one-allowance, biweekly, 2027 assumptions, this calculator's net as a share of gross is about 83% at $40,000, 82% at $50,000, 81% at $60,000, 79% at $75,000, 77% at $90,000, and 74% at $120,000. The $60,000 check in the table above is the 81% row: $1,864.28 is 80.8% of $2,307.69.

Those percentages are withholding plus FICA, with no health insurance and no 401(k). A real stub is often lower because benefits come out too. Quote "I keep 80%" only if you mean taxes and you are near this salary. A household that maxes a pre-tax retirement plan keeps a different deposit and owes a different tax.

The lines on the stub, in the order they bite

Start with gross. Subtract pre-tax items next, and be careful which ones touch which tax.

Section 125 benefits — the medical premium, a health FSA, an HSA taken through payroll — reduce the wage for federal income tax, Nebraska income tax, and FICA. A traditional 401(k) or 403(b) reduces the wage for the two income taxes and leaves FICA alone. You still pay 7.65% on the deferred dollar. A Roth 401(k), union dues, and a garnishment are post-tax. They shrink the deposit and do not shrink the tax.

Then FICA: 6.2% Social Security up to the wage base ($192,300 in 2027, $184,500 in 2026) and 1.45% Medicare on every dollar. Additional Medicare of 0.9% starts when wages from one employer pass $200,000. Then federal income tax from the W-4, using Publication 15-T. This calculator still uses the 2026 federal percentage-method worksheets for a 2027 pay date, because that is the edition loaded in the tool. Then Nebraska tax from the W-4N and the Circular EN table for the year on the pay date.

On the $60,000 biweekly example the order produces $193.08 federal, $176.54 FICA, and $73.80 Nebraska. FICA is larger than the state line. That surprises people who only watched the state rate fall. The state line got smaller. The 7.65% did not.

A married check is not half of two single checks

The same $60,000, paid biweekly in 2027, with married filing status and two W-4N allowances, withholds $109.23 federal and $59.88 Nebraska. FICA stays $176.54. Net is $1,962.04, against $1,864.28 for the single filer with one allowance.

The difference is about $98 a check, almost all of it income tax. FICA does not care that you are married. If both spouses work, do not paste this married result onto both jobs. Each employer withholds from the form it was given. Two "married, two allowances" forms can under-withhold the household, because each job behaves as if the other wage does not exist. The federal two-jobs checkbox and extra Nebraska withholding are the knobs. The Nebraska W-4N is about those knobs. This page is about reading the result.

What moves the deposit when the salary does not

Open enrollment is the quiet one. A premium that goes from $80 a paycheck to $110 is a $30 pay cut if it is pre-tax, and a slightly smaller tax bill that does not give the $30 back. People notice the premium and miss the tax offset. Both are real. The premium is larger.

A 401(k) increase does the same trick on the income-tax lines only. Raising a traditional deferral by $100 a check drops taxable wages by $100 and drops FICA wages by nothing. The deposit falls by $100 minus the income tax you no longer withhold. You did not lose the whole $100 to tax. You moved it to the account. Traditional and Roth side by side are in how a 401(k) changes Nebraska take-home pay.

Crossing the Social Security wage base does the opposite for high earners. Once year-to-date wages pass $192,300 in 2027, the 6.2% stops for the rest of the calendar year at that employer. Medicare continues. The check gets larger in the fall for a reason that has nothing to do with a raise. Two employers do not share a counter. Each withholds 6.2% until that employer's own wages hit the base, and you sort out the excess on the federal return.

A bonus paid with the regular check is just a fatter wage for that period. A bonus paid on its own can be withheld at a flat Nebraska rate of 3.5%, which is a withholding choice, not a higher tax. The return taxes it under the ordinary brackets. Federal supplemental withholding is its own rule and often 22% on that separate payment. Seeing 22% on a bonus and concluding the bonus is "taxed more" mixes a prepayment with a final rate. The two methods are in Nebraska bonus tax.

Refunds are a timing choice

A large Nebraska refund means the W-4N, or the extra-withholding line, sent in more than the return eventually charged. You can lower the prepayment by claiming the allowances that match the household, or by stopping an extra dollar amount you no longer want withheld. Each allowance on a 2027 biweekly check removes $96.54 from the wage before the table runs. On the $60,000 single example, moving from one allowance to two drops the state line from $73.80 to $70.01. That is about $4 a check, not a windfall, and it is the right size to expect at this wage.

Chasing a refund of several hundred dollars by claiming zero allowances is legal. It is also an interest-free loan. The bracket on the return does not improve because you withheld more. If you owed last year, the extra-withholding line is the cleaner fix, because allowances cannot see a side income that never hits this payroll.

First check, short check, three-check month

The first paycheck at a new job is often a partial period. If you started on a Wednesday, you may be paid for three days and still see benefit deductions that look full-size. Compare the hours or the salary fraction before you compare the tax. A tiny gross with a normal premium can even produce a near-zero or odd net. That is arithmetic, not a wrong rate.

A week you took unpaid leave should show fewer hours or a smaller salary amount. Tax falls with the wage. It does not stay glued to last paycheck's dollars. If the gross is right and the tax is wildly off, then look at filing status, allowances, and whether someone entered an extra withholding amount and forgot it.

Location inside Nebraska does not change this math. Omaha and Lincoln do not add a city wage tax. The cost of the apartment changes. The withholding table does not. City prices are a different guide.

Frequently asked questions

What percent of my paycheck goes to taxes in Nebraska?

For many middle-income single filers, total taxes (federal, FICA, and Nebraska) take about 18% to 25% of gross pay. Benefit deductions then reduce the deposited amount further.

Why is my first paycheck smaller?

Some employers pay in arrears, prorate the first period, or start benefit deductions immediately. A partial period can make the first check look unusually small even when your rate is correct.

Is a bonus taxed more in Nebraska?

Bonuses are supplemental wages. Federal withholding is often a flat 22%, and Nebraska supplemental withholding also applies.

The final tax is reconciled when you file, so the bonus is not really taxed at a higher rate overall.

Does pay frequency change total take-home?

No. Weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, and monthly schedules divide the same annual tax differently, but the yearly total is about the same.

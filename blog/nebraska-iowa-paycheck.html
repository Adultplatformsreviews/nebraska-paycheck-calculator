Nebraska vs Iowa Paycheck: Omaha and Council Bluffs

What changes on a 2027 stub when home and work sit on opposite sides of the river

The river changes the state line, not FICA

Omaha and Council Bluffs share a labor market and do not share a tax agency. Federal income tax withholding follows the same W-4 on either bank. Social Security is still 6.2% up to the 2027 wage base of $192,300, and Medicare is still 1.45%, because FICA is federal. What changes on the stub is the state income-tax line, and which state's return you file as a resident.

Neither city adds a city wage tax. Omaha's missing local tax is part of the Omaha and Lincoln paycheck guide, which is about Nebraska cities and the cost of living. This page is the commute that leaves Nebraska. Rent, groceries, and the bridge are not withholding. They also are not estimated here.

This site's calculators estimate Nebraska withholding. They will not produce an Iowa net pay, and this page will not invent one. If the employer is withholding Iowa tax, a Nebraska result is the wrong state.

There is no Nebraska–Iowa reciprocity

Iowa's only income-tax reciprocity agreement for wages is with Illinois. There is no matching agreement with Nebraska. Living in Council Bluffs and working in Omaha does not let the employer skip Nebraska withholding. Living in Omaha and working in Council Bluffs does not let the employer skip Iowa withholding. Reciprocity would have assigned the wage tax to the state where you live. That is what Iowa and Illinois do with each other. It is not what Iowa and Nebraska do.

Without reciprocity, the work state taxes the wages as income earned there, and the resident state taxes its residents on income from everywhere. The protection against paying both states in full on the same wages is a credit on the resident return, not a blank line on the stub. The credit is computed after the year, when both returns can see the tax that was actually paid. It is not a lower rate on Friday's check.

You work in Omaha and live in Council Bluffs

Nebraska taxes nonresidents on Nebraska-source wages. An Omaha employer generally withholds Nebraska income tax using Circular EN and the W-4N, the same booklet it uses for a coworker who lives in Millard. Your stub can look exactly like a Nebraska resident's stub. The address on the file does not, by itself, switch the table to Iowa.

You still have an Iowa resident return, because Iowa taxes residents on all income and then allows a credit for tax paid to another state on the same wages. Iowa's credit for taxes paid to another state is claimed on the Iowa return, on the out-of-state credit schedule. File the work-state side so you know the Nebraska tax on those wages before you claim the credit. The Nebraska withholding in box 17 of the W-2 is the prepayment toward the Nebraska nonresident return. It is not Iowa withholding.

A Nebraska estimate from the Nebraska paycheck calculator is useful here only as a picture of the Nebraska line the Omaha employer may withhold. It is not your Iowa balance, and it does not compute the credit. The difference between a stub and a resident return, in one state, is Nebraska Form 1040N vs paycheck withholding. Two states add a second return on top of that idea.

You work in Council Bluffs and live in Omaha

Iowa taxes the wages earned in Iowa. The employer withholds Iowa income tax if the wages are Iowa wages. Nebraska then taxes you as a resident on all income and allows a credit for the Iowa tax paid on those same wages. You are not done when the Iowa stub looks reasonable. The Nebraska return still exists, and the credit is what keeps the overlap from being paid twice in full.

Do not paste the Iowa gross into this site's calculator and read the Nebraska line as if Council Bluffs had withheld it. The tool will apply Circular EN. An Iowa employer is not applying Circular EN. If you want a Nebraska comparison for a different offer that really is a Nebraska job, run that offer on its own. Keep the two stubs separate.

Nonresidents of Iowa figure Iowa tax with Iowa's nonresident schedule so that Iowa-source income is what Iowa actually taxes. Wages for work performed in Iowa belong in that Iowa column. Interest from a Nebraska bank does not become Iowa wages because you drive across the bridge for work. The stub only ever showed the wages.

What Iowa's rate is, and what it is not

Iowa imposes individual income tax at 3.8% of Iowa taxable income. That flat rate took effect for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025, and it is the rate written into Iowa's income-tax statute. It replaced a graduated Iowa schedule. It is not 3.8% of gross pay. Iowa taxable income is what is left after Iowa's own deductions and exclusions. Withholding is an employer's prepayment toward that tax, using Iowa's withholding method, not Nebraska's allowance table.

Nebraska, on a 2027 paycheck, does not use a flat 3.8%. Circular EN's percentage-method rows for a 2027 pay date step from 2.26% through 4.03% after W-4N allowances. The return uses a different tier structure, which stays on the brackets guide. Comparing "Iowa is 3.8% and Nebraska is 3.99%" as if both numbers were the percent of gross on a stub is how border offers get misread. One of those styles of number is a flat statutory rate on taxable income. The other, 3.99%, is a top filing rate this site discusses elsewhere, not the withholding share of a typical gross.

For a concrete Nebraska side only: the usual $60,000 example, single, one allowance, biweekly, 2027, withholds $73.80 of Nebraska tax per check, or $1,918.80 across 26 checks, which is 3.20% of gross. That is a withholding share under Nebraska rules. It is not an Iowa result, and it is not a prediction of the credit.

School district surtax is not a city wage tax

Iowa school districts may levy a surtax that is a percent of Iowa income tax, not a percent of wages, and not a tax Omaha adds. It is tied to the district where you lived at the end of the year, and it is collected with the Iowa return. On the 2025 district list, Council Bluffs Community School District was 0% and Lewis Central, which covers part of that metro area, was 7% of Iowa income tax. A Council Bluffs street address does not tell you the district. The list is yearly, so a later year can differ. This page does not treat the 2025 list as a 2027 paycheck rate.

Because the surtax is a percent of Iowa income tax, a district at 0% adds nothing, and a district at 7% adds seven cents per dollar of Iowa income tax, not seven cents per dollar of wages. It still is not a line you should expect to see labeled "Council Bluffs wage tax." If your Iowa stub has only a state withholding line, that fits a system that settles the surtax on the return.

What is the same on both stubs

FICA is the same. A $2,000 biweekly gross under the wage base is $153 of employee FICA in Omaha or in Council Bluffs, because 7.65% does not stop at the river. The wage base, the additional 0.9% Medicare tax, and the fact that a traditional 401(k) does not reduce FICA are federal rules. They are written up in FICA Social Security and Medicare.

A traditional 401(k) deferral reduces wages for federal income tax and for the state that is taxing those wages. It does not reduce FICA in either state. The deposit arithmetic, measured on a Nebraska check, is how a 401(k) changes Nebraska take-home pay. An Iowa employer may withhold state tax on a similarly reduced wage. This site does not compute that Iowa line.

Social Security benefits are exempt from Nebraska income tax, and Iowa subtracts Social Security benefits that were included in federal income. The benefit is not the paycheck. Does Nebraska tax Social Security separates the benefit from the 6.2% wage tax. Moving from Omaha to Council Bluffs does not turn the benefit into wages, and it does not create reciprocity for the job.

Overtime, bonuses, and the wrong form

Overtime on a Nebraska hourly job is still Nebraska wages. The premium is not an Iowa tax and not a city tax. How a steady overtime week withholds in Nebraska is Nebraska overtime pay. An Iowa employer's overtime check follows Iowa withholding. The hours can look identical. The state line will not.

A bonus paid by an Omaha employer is Nebraska supplemental wages, including the option to withhold a flat 3.5% when the bonus is paid separately. That 3.5% is a Nebraska withholding election. It is not the Iowa rate. Nebraska bonus tax works the $5,000 example under Nebraska rules. Do not reuse 3.5% as "what Council Bluffs does." Iowa's flat tax rate on taxable income is 3.8%, and Iowa has its own withholding method for supplemental pay.

Giving an Iowa employer a Nebraska W-4N, or a Nebraska employer only a federal W-4, produces the default the form instructions warn about. Nebraska employers who do not receive a W-4N can withhold as single with zero allowances. That default is a Nebraska prepayment problem. It is not how you elect Iowa. The certificate itself is Nebraska W-4N.

How to compare two offers without a fake Iowa net

Write down where the work is performed, not where you sleep. If the work is in Nebraska, estimate the Nebraska line with the Nebraska paycheck calculator or, for an hourly rate, the hourly paycheck calculator. Set the pay date in 2027 when you want the 2027 Circular EN tables. Read federal tax, FICA, and Nebraska tax as a Nebraska paycheck.

If the work is in Iowa, keep those Nebraska dollars off the comparison, or label them clearly as "what a Nebraska employer would withhold on this gross," not as the Council Bluffs stub. Ask the Iowa employer which state it withholds, and read the first stub's state abbreviation before you budget the net. Then plan on two returns: the work state, and the resident state with a credit for the tax paid on the same wages.

A credit of this kind is limited to the tax the resident state would have charged on that same income and to the tax actually paid to the other state. The arithmetic happens on the returns. It does not happen in the Nebraska percentage method. The booklet is Nebraska Circular EN, and the Nebraska line alone is Nebraska tax withholding. Sales tax at the store is not on either stub.

Frequently asked questions

Do Nebraska and Iowa have wage reciprocity?

No. Iowa's only wage reciprocity agreement is with Illinois. A job in Omaha and a home in Council Bluffs, or the reverse, does not turn withholding off in the state where you work.

Does Council Bluffs add a city income tax to the paycheck?

No. Council Bluffs does not add a city wage tax, and neither does Omaha. The state line on the stub is Nebraska tax or Iowa tax, depending on where the wages are earned and how the employer withholds.

Can this site estimate an Iowa paycheck?

No. The calculators estimate Nebraska withholding, plus federal tax and FICA. An Iowa state line uses Iowa's own method. Do not read a Nebraska result as a Council Bluffs net pay.

Is FICA different in Iowa?

No. Social Security and Medicare are federal. The employee rates and the wage base do not change because you crossed the Missouri River.

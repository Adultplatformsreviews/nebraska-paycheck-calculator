Omaha and Lincoln Paycheck Guide: Taxes, Wages & Cost of Living

What your take-home pay goes toward in Nebraska's two largest cities

Same taxes, different costs

An Omaha paycheck and a Lincoln paycheck at the same salary are taxed the same way. Neither city has a local income tax, so withholding is federal tax, FICA, and Nebraska state tax only.

What differs between the cities is the cost of living: rent, sales tax at the register, commute, and property taxes for homeowners.

To get the actual net amount for your salary, use the Nebraska paycheck calculator; it applies to every Nebraska city. This page covers the context around that number.

Omaha

Omaha is the state's largest metro, with major employers in finance, insurance, healthcare, logistics, and engineering. The combined sales tax rate in Omaha is about 7% (5.5% state plus 1.5% city).

Median household income in the metro runs close to the national median, while housing costs stay below national averages, which makes take-home pay stretch further than in coastal cities.

Commuters from Council Bluffs, Iowa should note that wages earned in Nebraska are taxed by Nebraska; Iowa residents may then claim a credit on their Iowa return.

Lincoln

Lincoln, the state capital, is anchored by state government, the University of Nebraska, healthcare, and a growing tech sector. Its combined sales tax is about 7.25% after local options.

Rents are typically a bit lower than in Omaha, and many workers have shorter commutes.

Budgeting from net pay

A common guide is the 50/30/20 rule: half of take-home pay for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings and debt payoff. For example, a $55,000 salary in Omaha might produce roughly $1,700 to $1,800 per biweekly check after taxes, before benefits.

That is about $3,800 per month, so a needs budget near $1,900 has to cover rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, and transportation.

Other Nebraska cities

Grand Island, Kearney, Bellevue, Norfolk, and North Platte follow the same paycheck rules. Smaller cities often have lower rents and slightly different local sales tax rates.

Frequently asked questions

Does Omaha have a city income tax?

No. Omaha does not levy a local income or wage tax, and neither does Lincoln.

Only federal and Nebraska state taxes are withheld.

Is Omaha or Lincoln cheaper to live in?

Both are below the national average. Lincoln rents tend to be slightly lower, while Omaha has more job options and somewhat higher average wages, so the better fit depends on your field.

I live in Iowa and work in Omaha. Who taxes me?

Nebraska withholds on wages earned in Nebraska. You then file an Iowa return as a resident and claim a credit for tax paid to Nebraska to avoid double taxation.

What salary is comfortable in Omaha?

Many budget guides suggest that a single adult needs roughly $50,000 to $60,000 for a comfortable lifestyle in Omaha, depending on housing choices, debt, and savings goals.

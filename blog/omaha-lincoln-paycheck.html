Omaha and Lincoln Paycheck Guide: Taxes, Wages & Cost of Living

What your take-home pay goes toward in Nebraska's two largest cities

Same taxes, different costs

An Omaha paycheck and a Lincoln paycheck at the same salary are taxed the same way. Neither city has a local income tax, so withholding is federal tax, FICA, and Nebraska state tax only.

What differs between the cities is the cost of living: rent, sales tax at the register, commute, and property taxes for homeowners.

To get the actual net amount for your salary, use the Nebraska paycheck calculator; it applies to every Nebraska city. This page covers the context around that number.

Omaha

Omaha is the state's largest metro, with major employers in finance, insurance, healthcare, logistics, and engineering. The combined sales tax rate in Omaha is about 7% (5.5% state plus 1.5% city).

Housing is the line that usually decides whether a given deposit feels like enough. This guide does not print a metro-wide rent or a median income. Use the rent you were quoted.

Commuters from Council Bluffs, Iowa should note that wages earned in Nebraska are taxed by Nebraska; Iowa residents may then claim a credit on their Iowa return.

Lincoln

Lincoln, the state capital, is anchored by state government, the University of Nebraska, healthcare, and a growing tech sector. Its combined sales tax is about 7.25% after local options.

Rents are typically a bit lower than in Omaha, and many workers have shorter commutes.

Budgeting from net pay

A common guide is the 50/30/20 rule: half of take-home pay for needs, 30% for wants, and 20% for savings and debt payoff.

For example, a $55,000 salary in Omaha might produce roughly $1,700 to $1,800 per biweekly check after taxes, before benefits.

That is about $3,800 per month, so a needs budget near $1,900 has to cover rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, and transportation.

Other Nebraska cities

Grand Island, Kearney, Bellevue, Norfolk, and North Platte follow the same paycheck rules. Smaller cities often have lower rents and slightly different local sales tax rates.

If you are paid by the hour, try the hourly paycheck calculator. For state withholding details, read Nebraska tax withholding.

The paycheck is the same math in both cities

Take a salary, a filing status, and a W-4N. Run them once. You can hand the result to someone in Omaha or someone in Lincoln and it is still the right withholding estimate. Neither city has a local income tax or a local wage tax. The lines on the stub are federal income tax, FICA, and Nebraska income tax.

What changes is everything that happens after the deposit: sales tax at the register, rent, how far you drive, and whether you are also paying Iowa tax because you live across the river. This page is about that split. The calculator is about the deposit. Use the Nebraska paycheck calculator for the number, and stay here for what the number has to cover.

One deposit, then the month

A single filer earning $55,000, one W-4N allowance, no pre-tax benefits, paid every two weeks, pay date in 2027, nets about $1,717.31 on this calculator. Gross on that check is $2,115.38. Federal withholding is $170.00, FICA is $161.83, and Nebraska tax is $66.24.

Twenty-six of those net checks are about $44,650 a year, or about $3,721 a month. Half of $3,721 is about $1,860. That is the "needs" half if you use the 50/30/20 sketch: needs, wants, and savings. It is a sketch, not a rule of the state. Rent, utilities, groceries, insurance, and a car payment all have to live inside whatever share you actually assign to needs. A benefit premium you forgot to subtract will already have made the $1,717 smaller.

The same salary in either city produces that deposit. A one-bedroom that costs more in one city than the other is not a tax difference. It is the reason two people with identical stubs feel differently paid on the first of the month.

Sales tax is where the cities diverge

State sales tax is 5.5% everywhere in Nebraska. Omaha's combined rate is about 7%. Lincoln's combined rate is about 7.25%. On a $100 taxable purchase that is about $7.00 in Omaha and about $7.25 in Lincoln. Groceries are generally exempt, so the gap shows up on restaurant meals, household goods, and other taxable sales, not on every bag of food.

Twenty-five cents on $100 will not decide which city you live in. It does explain why a cost-of-living article and a paycheck article are not the same piece. The stub never shows sales tax. The receipt does.

Omaha, including the Iowa side of the bridge

Omaha is the larger job market: insurance, finance, health care, transportation, and a long list of employers that are not a single industry. Pay frequency is whatever that employer uses. A biweekly check and a semi-monthly check are both common, and they are not the same size even at the same salary. The take-home guide shows a $60,000 salary cut four ways. The city does not pick the frequency for you.

Bellevue and the rest of Sarpy County sit in the same metro for a lot of commutes. Withholding is still Nebraska withholding if the wage is a Nebraska wage. Offutt is a large employer next door. Military pay has its own federal rules, and a nonresident servicemember's spouse may file Form 9N so Nebraska tax is not withheld on the spouse's wages. That is a certificate, not a city tax.

Living in Council Bluffs and working in Omaha is the case that actually changes the return. Nebraska can withhold on the Nebraska wage. Iowa, as the state where you live, taxes its residents and generally allows a credit for tax paid to another state so the same dollar is not fully taxed twice. The credit is claimed on the Iowa return. It is not a second line on the Nebraska stub. This calculator estimates Nebraska withholding. It does not prepare the Iowa credit.

Lincoln

State government and the university anchor a lot of Lincoln payrolls, alongside hospitals and private employers. A university or state pay calendar can be monthly or biweekly depending on the job. The tax math does not care which building you badge into. It cares about the gross, the frequency, and the forms.

Rents in Lincoln are often a bit lower than comparable places in Omaha, and a lot of commutes are shorter. "Often" is doing real work in that sentence. A specific apartment can invert it. Do not budget from a proverb. Take the net from the calculator, subtract the rent you were actually quoted, and see what is left for a car, groceries, and the sales tax on the things that are taxable.

Property tax matters more if you buy. Nebraska's property-tax credits can lower the bill you pay the county. They never appear as a negative line on a paycheck. A homeowner in either city who compares "take-home" and forgets the escrow payment is comparing the wrong pair of numbers.

Other towns, same withholding

Grand Island, Kearney, Norfolk, North Platte, and Bellevue follow the same paycheck rules. Local sales-tax add-ons differ. Rents often run lower than in the two big metros, which is a housing fact, not a tax fact. A $20-an-hour job withholds the same federal, FICA, and Nebraska amounts in Kearney as it does in Omaha if the hours, the forms, and the benefits match.

Someone choosing between a slightly higher hourly rate in Omaha and a lower rent in a smaller city should compare deposits, not job titles. Run both rates on the hourly calculator, turn them into a monthly net, and set them next to the two rents. The tax tables will not break the tie. The rent will.

What people mean by a comfortable salary

There is no official "comfortable" wage for Omaha or Lincoln. Households differ by debt, childcare, and whether anyone else contributes a paycheck. The $55,000 example above is about $3,721 a month before benefits for one person. If rent is near half of that, the 50/30/20 sketch is already strained, and the strain is the rent, not a hidden city tax.

Two incomes change the month faster than a tax tweak does. They also change withholding, because two jobs are two forms. Get the deposit right with the calculator, then decide if the city is affordable. Doing it backwards — picking the city, then hoping the stub has a local discount — does not work here. There is no local discount. There is also no local surcharge.

An hourly deposit, same in both cities

$20 an hour, 40 hours a week, single, one allowance, no benefits, paid every two weeks, pay date in 2027: gross $1,600, net about $1,323.36 on this calculator. Twenty-six checks are about $34,407 a year, or about $2,867 a month. Omaha and Lincoln produce the same figures. The apartment does not.

Sales tax nibbles at what you buy after that deposit. On $500 of taxable purchases in a month, about 7% in Omaha is $35 and about 7.25% in Lincoln is $36.25. Groceries, generally exempt, are not in that $500 if you kept them out. The gap is a dollar and change. Rent gaps are hundreds. People still argue the sales tax because it is printed on the receipt and the rent difference is something they only notice after they have already signed.

Commuting in from Papillion, La Vista, Gretna, or Waverly does not create a new withholding rate. If the employer is paying Nebraska wages, the stub uses Nebraska tables. The miles change the car cost. They do not change Circular EN. Crossing into Council Bluffs does change the state, which is Nebraska vs Iowa paycheck.

A household with two stubs

Two paychecks in one apartment are not twice one paycheck's tax. Each employer withholds from the form it received. Two single-looking forms in a married household, or two full allowance claims, can leave the April return short. The city still does not add a tax. The fix is on the W-4 and the W-4N, and it is the same fix in Douglas County and in Lancaster County.

Run each job through the calculator separately if you want each deposit. Add the nets yourself for the month. Do not type the combined salary into one calculation and expect either stub to match, unless one employer is paying the whole amount. The Nebraska W-4N is the form. This page is the reminder that moving from Lincoln to Omaha will not repair a bad allowance count.

Frequently asked questions

Does Omaha have a city income tax?

No. Omaha does not levy a local income or wage tax, and neither does Lincoln.

Only federal and Nebraska state taxes are withheld.

Is Omaha or Lincoln cheaper to live in?

Withholding is the same in both cities. Lincoln rents are often a bit lower, and Omaha has the larger job market. A specific apartment can reverse the rent comparison, so set the rent you were quoted next to the same net paycheck.

I live in Iowa and work in Omaha. Who taxes me?

Nebraska withholds on wages earned in Nebraska. You then file an Iowa return as a resident and claim a credit for tax paid to Nebraska to avoid double taxation.

What salary is comfortable in Omaha?

There is no official figure. On this calculator, $55,000 for a single filer with one allowance, no pre-tax benefits, paid biweekly on a 2027 pay date, nets about $1,717 a check, or about $3,721 a month. Comfortable is whatever is left after the rent you were actually quoted.

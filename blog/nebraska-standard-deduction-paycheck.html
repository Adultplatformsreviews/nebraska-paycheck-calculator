Nebraska Standard Deduction and Paycheck Withholding

A Form 1040N subtraction the stub never prints

The return subtracts it, and the stub has no line for it

The Nebraska standard deduction is a subtraction on Form 1040N. Federal adjusted gross income comes onto the return, Nebraska adjustments are applied, and the deduction comes off before Nebraska taxable income is left for the rate schedule. Paycheck withholding never prints a line called "standard deduction." Circular EN subtracts W-4N allowances from the wage and looks up a table. Those are different subtractions, in different documents, and they are not sized to match.

That is why a worker can look at a full year of stubs, add up Nebraska withholding, and still see a refund or a balance due that the stubs cannot explain by themselves. The deduction is one of the return items the stubs do not apply. The timing story, prepayment versus the bill, is Nebraska Form 1040N vs paycheck withholding. The rates that apply after the deduction are Nebraska income tax brackets. This page stops at the deduction.

The amounts on the 2025 return and the 2026 worksheet

The 2025 Form 1040N instructions print these standard deductions when no age or blindness boxes are checked. A 2027 indexed amount is not in this site's withholding tables, and it is not printed here. The 2026 Form 1040N-ES worksheet is the later set of base amounts: $8,850 single, $17,700 married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse, $8,850 married filing separately, and $12,950 head of household.

Use the form for the year you are filing. A paycheck dated in 2027 still will not subtract whichever of these numbers is current. The withholding tables do not look the deduction up. If you are sizing a 2027 return before that year's instruction booklet is the one in your hand, the honest statement is that the deduction will be the indexed amount on that booklet, and the check in your account will have ignored it either way.

Someone who can be claimed on another person's return for the federal child tax credit or the dependent credit uses a smaller figure: the federal standard deduction actually allowed, or the chart amount, whichever is less. A teenager with a part-time Nebraska job does not automatically get the full single amount. The paycheck still will not show the deduction. It will show whatever the W-4N told the employer to do.

Age and blindness add to the chart

The 2025 instructions add $2,000 for each age or blindness box checked by a single filer or a head of household, and $1,650 for each box on a married filing jointly return. That is why the 2025 single column runs $8,600, $10,600, and $12,600, and the joint column runs $17,200, $18,850, $20,500, $22,150, and $23,800. Married filing separately uses the spouse's boxes only when a personal exemption is allowed for the spouse. The 2025 separate column runs $8,600, $10,250, $11,900, $13,550, and $15,200.

The 2026 estimated-tax worksheet raises those add-ons. At the end of 2026, an unmarried person, single or head of household, adds $2,050 if 65 or older or blind, and $4,100 if both. A qualifying surviving spouse adds $1,700 or $3,400 on that same split. A married person adds $1,700 if 65 or older or blind, and $3,400 if both. When both spouses qualify, the worksheet stacks further amounts. Married filing separately uses the spouse amounts only if a personal exemption is allowed for the spouse. The worksheet for the year is the list to follow once both spouses are in the picture. This page does not reprint every combination.

None of those add-ons are a W-4N allowance. Claiming an extra allowance because someone turned 65 is a withholding choice. The return still adds the standard-deduction amount if the boxes apply, whether or not the allowance was claimed. The allowance guide is Nebraska W-4N. It owns the count. This page owns the deduction.

$60,000 of wages and an $8,850 subtraction

Take the reference wage this site uses everywhere: $60,000, no other income, no Nebraska adjustment. Using the 2026 single standard deduction of $8,850 as an illustration, Nebraska income before adjustments is $60,000 minus $8,850, or $51,150. That sentence is arithmetic on a published deduction. It is not a 2027 filed return, and it is not the tax. The rate schedule that applies to taxable income after this point lives on the brackets guide. Quoting a percentage here would turn this page into a second rate sheet.

The same worker's 2027 biweekly stub does not subtract $8,850. Gross is $2,307.69. One W-4N allowance removes $96.54. The Circular EN table then withholds $73.80 of Nebraska tax. Across 26 checks that is $1,918.80, which is 3.20% of gross wages. The rate guide calls a figure like that a withholding share, and it points out that a single worker near this wage often lands nearer an effective rate around 3% once the return's deduction and the progressive schedule have done their work. The effective-rate sentence stays on Nebraska state tax rate. The $96.54 allowance stays on Nebraska Circular EN.

The $8,600 federal figure is the adjustment in the percentage-method worksheets this calculator still uses for a 2027 pay date. It is a federal withholding mechanic. It is not the Nebraska standard deduction, even though the two numbers sit near each other. People who "see" a standard deduction in the federal line and expect the Nebraska line to mirror it are looking at the wrong document. Nebraska withholding uses the allowance. The Nebraska return uses the deduction.

Itemizing federally, and the state tax that comes back out

If you take the standard deduction on the federal return, you take the Nebraska standard deduction on the Nebraska return. If you itemize on the federal return, Nebraska lets you take the larger of the Nebraska standard deduction or your federal itemized deductions minus state and local income taxes. The Nebraska tax withheld on the stub is the kind of state income tax that subtraction removes. A year of Nebraska withholding does not become a Nebraska itemized deduction.

That rule surprises people who paid a lot of state tax through the year and expect the state tax to lower the state tax. The form pulls state and local income taxes out of the itemized figure, including the amount reported on the federal schedule even when a federal cap already limited the deduction that reached the federal total. Motor vehicle tax based on the value of the vehicle, the amount paid at registration, is the personal-property piece Nebraska's instructions allow. Sales tax at the store is not a payroll deduction and it is not this standard deduction.

After the larger of the two Nebraska figures is taken, the return computes tax, and then a personal exemption credit comes off the tax. The 2025 instructions use $171 per Nebraska personal exemption. The 2026 Form 1040N prints $176. That credit is not part of the standard deduction. A child care credit is a later line still, and it is Nebraska child care tax credit. Stacking those credits into the deduction double-counts them.

Why the deduction can show up as a refund

Withholding on the $60,000 example sends $1,918.80 to Nebraska over the year. The return starts from adjusted gross income, subtracts the standard deduction or the Nebraska itemized amount, and applies credits the paycheck never computed. If the tax after those steps is less than $1,918.80, the difference is a Nebraska refund. The deduction is one reason that can happen. It is not a promise of a refund, and a refund is not a lower bracket. You prepaid more than the return charged.

One extra W-4N allowance will not stand in for an $8,850 deduction. On this wage the allowance guide measures one allowance at about four dollars of Nebraska tax per biweekly check, on the order of $99 a year. The deduction removes thousands of dollars of income before any rate is applied. Different object, different scale. Changing allowances to "claim the standard deduction" misnames both. Allowances change the prepayment. The deduction changes the tax base on the return whether you claim one allowance or six.

A pre-tax HSA or health FSA lowers the wages in the W-2, so federal adjusted gross income is already smaller before the standard deduction comes off. The deduction still applies. It does not replace the cafeteria reduction, and the cafeteria reduction does not replace the deduction. The paycheck side of those accounts is HSA and health FSA on a Nebraska paycheck. Side income that never hits the stub also reaches the return before the deduction. Prepaying that income is Nebraska estimated tax payments. The deduction does not withhold itself.

What to type, and what not to type, in the calculator

The Nebraska paycheck calculator estimates withholding. It has no field for the Nebraska standard deduction, because the employer does not ask for one. Leaving every deduction field blank on the $60,000 example is how you get the $73.80 state line. Typing $8,850 into pre-tax benefits would pretend the deduction was a cafeteria plan. FICA would fall, and a real stub would not do that for a standard deduction. Do not do it.

The federal worksheets inside the tool already include the Publication 15-T adjustment described above. You do not add the federal standard deduction on top. Adding it again understates federal withholding. Nebraska tax withholding is the screen for the Nebraska line alone. It uses allowances, not the Form 1040N chart.

Married filing jointly uses the joint deduction on the return and, if that is the household's only wage, often a different allowance count on the W-4N. The stub still will not show $17,700 or $17,200 coming off one check. Two jobs in one household make the mismatch larger, because each employer withholds as if it sees the only wage, and the return applies one deduction to the combined income. Fixing that with allowances or extra withholding is the W-4N guide. The deduction did not cause the two-job problem. The two employers did.

Frequently asked questions

Does Nebraska paycheck withholding subtract the standard deduction?

No. Withholding subtracts W-4N allowances and then uses the Circular EN table. The Nebraska standard deduction is subtracted on Form 1040N.

What is the Nebraska standard deduction?

On the 2025 Form 1040N instructions the base amounts are $8,600 single or married filing separately, $17,200 married filing jointly or qualifying surviving spouse, and $12,600 head of household. The 2026 estimated-tax worksheet uses $8,850, $17,700, and $12,950. A 2027 indexed amount is not printed on this page.

Is a W-4N allowance the same as the standard deduction?

No. One allowance in 2027 removes $2,510 from annualized wages, which is $96.54 on a biweekly check. The standard deduction is a larger subtraction on the return, and it applies whether or not you claimed the allowance.

If I take the federal standard deduction, what does Nebraska use?

The Nebraska standard deduction. Nebraska itemized deductions are available only if you itemized on the federal return, and state income taxes are subtracted before Nebraska compares that figure with the standard deduction.

How to Read a Nebraska Pay Stub

By Nebraska Paycheck Calculator · Updated November 9, 2026

Current and year-to-date columns, the tax lines, and the deductions that are not Nebraska tax

Start with the two columns

A Nebraska pay stub is two stories on one page. The current column is this pay period only. The year-to-date column is every check this employer has paid you since January 1, including this one. Most arguments about a "wrong rate" start by comparing a year-to-date tax with this week's gross.

Read current against current, and year-to-date against year-to-date. A year-to-date Nebraska line that looks huge next to one week of pay is usually the sum of many checks. Divide year-to-date tax by year-to-date gross if you want a share of wages so far. Do not divide year-to-date tax by this check's gross.

The pay date printed on the stub chooses the Nebraska table. A check dated in 2027 uses the 2027 withholding tables. A check dated in 2026 still uses the 2026 tables, even if you are looking at the PDF in 2027. Social Security uses a wage base of $192,300 for 2027 wages and $184,500 for 2026 wages. The rate pair, 6.2% and 1.45%, does not change with that date. The cap does.

Whose check this is

Before any dollar, confirm the employer name, your name, the period start, the period end, and how often you are paid. Weekly pay is 52 checks. Biweekly pay is 26. Semi-monthly pay is 24. Monthly pay is 12. A biweekly salary check is annual pay divided by 26. A semi-monthly check is divided by 24 and looks larger. That difference is the calendar, not a raise.

If the frequency on the stub is not the frequency you think you have, every later comparison is aimed at the wrong check. People who moved from weekly to biweekly often keep last year's weekly net in their head and decide the new tax is heavier. The deposit is larger because the period is longer. The year's tax is about the same. The Nebraska take-home pay guide shows the same salary cut four ways.

Earnings before anything is withheld

Hourly stubs list a rate, regular hours, and sometimes overtime hours at another rate. Salary stubs list a salary amount for the period. Gross pay is those earnings before withholding. Paid time off, holiday hours, and a shift differential belong in that earnings block when they were paid. They are wages. They are not a separate Nebraska tax category.

A partial first period is a smaller gross. Compare hours, or the fraction of the salary period, before you compare tax. A short gross with a full insurance premium can make net look broken when the earnings line is the line that changed. Unpaid leave should shrink hours or salary. Tax falls with the wage. It does not stay glued to last check's dollars.

This site's reference check is a single filer paid $60,000 a year, biweekly, with one W-4N allowance, no pre-tax deductions, and a pay date in 2027. The calculator prints this current column. Your employer's labels will be shorter. The dollars are an estimate for that setup, not a promise that every payroll system matches to the cent.

Social Security and Medicare together are $176.54, which is 7.65% of $2,307.69. That pair is the FICA block. Stubs often print the two lines and never print the word FICA.

Names on the tax lines

Federal income tax may be labeled FIT, Federal, or US Withholding. It comes from the federal W-4 and the percentage method in Publication 15-T. This calculator still uses the 2026 federal worksheets for a 2027 pay date, because that is the edition loaded in the tool. Federal tax is not a flat percent of gross. It moves when the W-4 moves. FICA does not.

Social Security is often OASDI or SS, at 6.2% of FICA wages up to the wage base. Medicare is often MED or HI, at 1.45% of FICA wages with no wage-base cap. Add those two boxes when you want the 7.65% test. Do not add the federal box into that test. Additional Medicare, an extra 0.9%, shows up only after wages from that employer pass $200,000. Most stubs never have the line. The longer explanation of the cap and the extra 0.9% is FICA Social Security and Medicare.

Nebraska income tax may be labeled NE, SIT, or State. It comes from Form W-4N and the Circular EN table for the year on the pay date. One allowance on a 2027 biweekly check removes $96.54 from the wage before that table runs. How many allowances to claim is the Nebraska W-4N guide. The arithmetic is Nebraska tax withholding. What the booklet is, as opposed to the form you file in April, is Nebraska Circular EN.

The local line should be zero

Nebraska has no city or county wage tax. Omaha, Lincoln, Grand Island, Bellevue, and a small town all use the same state calculation. A local-income-tax line on a Nebraska stub should read $0.00. If a city tax is being withheld, the work location or the employer's tax setup needs a question. It is not a new Nebraska rate.

Sales tax and property tax never appear as payroll deductions. They show up at the store and on the house, which is a different subject. City prices for Omaha and Lincoln are in the Omaha and Lincoln paycheck guide. A commute across the river into Council Bluffs is a different state's withholding, covered in Nebraska vs Iowa paycheck.

Deductions, and which tax they touch

Pre-tax lines sit in their own block or above the taxes. A traditional 401(k) or 403(b) reduces the wage for federal and Nebraska income tax and does not reduce Social Security or Medicare. A Roth 401(k) is post-tax. It lowers the deposit and does not lower those taxes. Health, dental, and vision premiums run through a cafeteria plan, a health FSA, and an HSA taken from payroll usually reduce income tax and FICA. Read the label before you decide the 7.65% test failed.

The paycheck effect of the retirement line, traditional against Roth, is in how a 401(k) changes Nebraska take-home pay. Post-tax lines come out after tax: Roth deferrals, union dues, garnishments, and some voluntary insurance. A garnishment is not Nebraska tax. Folding it into the state line invents a rate the table does not have.

Employer-paid items do not belong as employee deductions. Nebraska unemployment tax and FUTA are employer costs. The employer's FICA match is not subtracted from your net. If a line labeled unemployment is reducing the deposit, ask. That split is in Nebraska payroll taxes. An employer 401(k) match, when it appears, is usually a memo of what was added to the account. It is not a second subtraction from net.

Year-to-date is a sum

After 10 identical biweekly checks at the $60,000 example, year-to-date gross is 10 times $2,307.69, or $23,076.90. Year-to-date federal tax, Social Security, Medicare, and Nebraska tax are the sums of the amounts already withheld. They are not a fresh calculation on $23,076.90 at a new rate. Pennies follow the employer's rounding on each check. Do not expect the year-to-date net to be a perfectly neat multiple if one check in the stack was a partial period.

The Social Security cap is a year-to-date test at that employer. In 2027 the 6.2% stops on later checks once year-to-date Social Security wages pass $192,300. A larger net in the fall is often that cap, not a raise. Medicare continues. A second employer does not see the first employer's counter and starts the 6.2% again. Two stubs can both look "right" and still have withheld too much Social Security for the year. That excess is a federal credit, not a Nebraska refund.

Overtime, a bonus, and paid leave

Overtime hours belong in the earnings block at the rate you were paid. Nebraska does not add a special overtime tax on top of ordinary withholding. Time-and-a-half is still wages for FICA, federal income tax, and Nebraska income tax. A steady overtime schedule and a single heavy week are different problems for any estimator that annualizes a week. The Nebraska overtime pay guide is that distinction. The hourly paycheck calculator is where the hours go.

A bonus paid inside the regular check is extra gross on that stub. A bonus paid alone can show a flat Nebraska withholding rate of 3.5% and a flat federal rate, often 22%. Those are withholding methods. They are not a separate Nebraska bonus tax. The two methods, with a $5,000 example, are in Nebraska bonus tax.

Vacation, sick, and holiday pay are wages when they are paid. Hourly workers should see the hours that were actually paid. If those hours are missing, fix the gross before you blame a tax rate. Reported tips belong in the earnings block too. The tax on them is tip withholding on a Nebraska paycheck.

When the stub and the estimate disagree

Match gross first. Hours times rate, or salary divided by the number of periods, including overtime. If gross is wrong, every tax line will be wrong in a way that looks like a bad rate.

Then match FICA. If Social Security plus Medicare is far from 7.65% of the FICA wage, a Section 125 benefit may have reduced the wage, you may be over the Social Security cap, or the wage you divided is not the wage on the stub. Traditional 401(k) deferrals do not explain a smaller FICA line.

Then match Nebraska. Filing status, allowance count, extra withholding, and the pay date all move that line. A gap of a few dollars can be wage-bracket tables versus the percentage method, or rounding. A gap the size of a whole other tax usually means a different form, a benefit you did not enter, or a 2026 stub compared with a 2027 estimate.

Two jobs, two stubs

Each employer withholds from the forms it was given. Two stubs can each be internally consistent and still under-withhold the household, because neither job sees the other wage. Adding the two nets is the right way to see the month's deposits. Adding the two salaries into one calculator run will not reproduce either stub.

Year-to-date figures do not combine across employers. Excess Social Security tax from two jobs is reconciled on the federal return. Nebraska does not run a second wage base. When a later check looks strange, compare the current columns and find the line that moved.

Frequently asked questions

What is year-to-date on a Nebraska pay stub?

Year-to-date is the total this employer has already paid you in the calendar year, including the current check. Read it against other year-to-date figures. It is not this week's gross multiplied by a new tax rate.

Why is there no city tax on my Nebraska pay stub?

Nebraska does not have a local wage tax. Omaha, Lincoln, and other Nebraska cities do not add a city income tax to the check. Sales tax and property tax are not lines on the stub.

Which labels mean Social Security and Medicare?

Social Security is often OASDI or SS. Medicare is often MED or HI. Under the wage base, with no pre-tax benefit reducing FICA wages, the two add to 7.65% of the FICA wage.

Does a 401(k) line lower every tax on the stub?

A traditional 401(k) lowers federal and Nebraska income-tax withholding. It does not lower Social Security or Medicare. A Roth 401(k) lowers the deposit and does not lower those taxes.

How Nebraska paycheck taxes work

A Nebraska paycheck tax calculator follows one order of operations. That order also shows why two people with the same salary can take home different amounts.

01
Start with gross pay

Annual salary divided by the number of pay periods, or hourly rate × hours × 52 split by frequency. Overtime, bonuses, and commissions raise gross pay for the period they are paid.

02
Subtract pre-tax deductions

Traditional 401(k) or 403(b) contributions, HSA deposits, health FSA elections, and pre-tax medical premiums come off before income tax is figured. Section 125 items such as premiums and FSA also usually reduce FICA wages.

03
Withhold federal income tax

Your employer uses IRS Publication 15-T and your federal W-4 (filing status, dependents, extra withholding). This calculator approximates that with the standard deduction and current brackets.

04
Withhold FICA

Social Security takes 6.2% of wages up to the annual wage base, and Medicare takes 1.45% of all wages, plus an extra 0.9% on wages above $200,000.

05
Withhold Nebraska state income tax

NDOR Circular EN and your Form W-4N set this line. A 2027 pay date uses graduated rates of about 2.26% to 4.03%.

A 2026 pay date still uses tables that top out near 4.60%.

06
Subtract post-tax deductions

Roth 401(k) contributions, union dues, wage garnishments, and voluntary insurance come out last. What remains is your net pay.

Federal vs. Nebraska state vs. FICA vs. local tax

Federal income taxProgressive brackets from 10% to 37% on income above the standard deduction. Usually the largest variable withholding line.
FICA (7.65%)Social Security 6.2% plus Medicare 1.45%. A flat rate that does not depend on your W-4 or W-4N.
Nebraska income taxNDOR Circular EN rates of about 2.26%–4.03% on a 2027 pay date, after W-4N allowances. A 2026 pay date still tops out near 4.60%.

Local tax is $0. Unlike Ohio, Pennsylvania, or Kentucky, Nebraska cities and counties do not levy a municipal wage or occupational income tax on typical employee pay.

That is why the local line in the calculator always reads $0.00, whether you work in Omaha, Lincoln, Grand Island, or a small town. Local sales and property taxes exist, but they never show up on a pay stub.

In practice, FICA is predictable, federal tax depends heavily on filing status and pre-tax savings, and Nebraska tax is a modest but meaningful slice, often 3%–4% of gross for middle incomes.

Biweekly pay is the most common schedule in the state. It produces two extra paychecks a year compared with twice-a-month pay, and those three-payday months are useful for savings.

Hourly paycheck

Hourly workers multiply their rate by weekly hours. Hours can change, so many people model a typical week and a busy week.

Semi-monthly vs. biweekly

Semi-monthly pay (24 checks, usually on the 15th and last day of the month) gives slightly larger checks than biweekly (26 checks) for the same salary.

Monthly budgeting is simpler on a semi-monthly schedule, while biweekly checks line up with weekly hourly work.

Deductions, withholdings, and net pay

Net pay is gross pay minus taxes and deductions. Where a deduction falls in that order matters as much as its size.

Pre-tax deductions (lower your taxes)

Traditional 401(k) and 403(b) contributions reduce federal and Nebraska taxable wages, but not FICA. HSA contributions through payroll and health FSA elections under a cafeteria plan usually reduce federal, state, and FICA wages.

Pre-tax medical, dental, and vision premiums work the same way. Enter the annual total in the "Pre-tax deductions" field.

As a rule of thumb, each $100 of pre-tax savings costs a middle-income Nebraskan only about $70–$75 of take-home pay.

Post-tax deductions (no tax benefit now)

Roth 401(k) contributions, after-tax life or disability insurance, union dues, charitable payroll giving, and garnishments come out after all taxes. Enter these per paycheck in the "Post-tax deductions" field.

Adjusting withholding

If you get a big refund every spring, you are over-withholding. You could raise W-4N allowances or adjust your federal W-4 to increase each check.

If you owe, add extra withholding instead. The "federal credits" field lets you model annual dependent credits from Step 3 of the W-4.

Omaha, Lincoln, and remote workers

Because Nebraska has no local wage tax, an Omaha paycheck and a Lincoln paycheck at the same pay are taxed the same way. NDOR does not add a city withholding line, so one state tool covers every city.

The one common cross-border case is the Omaha–Council Bluffs metro.

Iowa residents working in Nebraska, and Nebraska residents working in Iowa, may have withholding for one state and file in both, with a credit for taxes paid to the other state.

Remote employees generally have withholding for the state where they physically do the work. Rules depend on your facts, so confirm with your employer or a tax professional.

Nebraska paycheck calculator FAQs

How do I estimate taxes out of my paycheck in Nebraska?

Take gross pay, subtract pre-tax deductions, then withhold federal income tax, Social Security (6.2%), Medicare (1.45%), and Nebraska income tax based on your W-4N. The calculator above does all of these steps at once.

What taxes come out of a Nebraska paycheck?

Federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and Nebraska state income tax. There is no local or city income tax in Nebraska.

How much is $60,000 a year after taxes in Nebraska?

For a single filer paid biweekly with one W-4N allowance and no deductions, a 2026 pay date is about $1,856 per check. The calculator opens on a 2027 pay date, which is about $1,864 per check because Circular EN 2027 withholds less Nebraska tax.

Federal income tax still follows the 2026 Publication 15-T schedules.

How much is $63,000 a year after taxes in Nebraska?

For a single filer paid biweekly with one W-4N allowance and no deductions, a 2027 pay date is about $1,952 per check, or about $50,764 a year. Gross on that check is about $2,423 before federal income tax, FICA, and Nebraska withholding.

Does Nebraska have a city or local income tax?

No. Omaha, Lincoln, and other Nebraska cities do not withhold a local wage tax from employee paychecks.

What is the Nebraska state income tax rate on paychecks?

Withholding follows graduated Circular EN rates, about 2.26% to 4.60% for a 2026 pay date and about 2.26% to 4.03% for a 2027 pay date, after W-4N allowances. See Nebraska tax withholding for the bracket walkthrough.

Is this a Nebraska paycheck calculator with taxes and deductions?

Yes. It includes federal, FICA, and state taxes plus fields for pre-tax and post-tax deductions, so the net figure reflects both.

How is a biweekly paycheck calculated?

Annual pay is divided by 26. Annual tax estimates are divided the same way, so each biweekly check carries 1/26 of the year's withholding.

Can I calculate an hourly paycheck here?

Yes, switch to Hourly. For hourly-specific examples and overtime tips, use the hourly paycheck calculator.

How does a 401(k) change my Nebraska take-home pay?

Traditional 401(k) contributions lower federal and Nebraska taxable wages, so net pay drops by less than the amount you contribute. Roth contributions do not lower current taxes. The line-by-line effect is in how a 401(k) changes Nebraska take-home pay.

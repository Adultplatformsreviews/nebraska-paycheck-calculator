Hourly wage to Nebraska take-home pay

If you are paid by the hour, your paycheck moves with your schedule. This Nebraska hourly paycheck calculator starts in Hourly mode.

Enter your rate and typical weekly hours, choose how often you are paid, and see estimated take-home pay after federal tax, FICA, and Nebraska income tax.

Hourly pay is annualized as rate × hours × 52, taxed on an annual basis, then split back into weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly checks. Hourly pay varies, so it helps to run a slow week and a busy week to get a realistic range.

Nebraska minimum wage context

The Nebraska Department of Labor schedule sets the minimum wage at $15.26 per hour on January 1, 2027, after a $15.00 rate in 2026. Full-time at $15.26 is about $31,741 a year before taxes and, in the table above, about $516 on a weekly check for a single filer.

Tipped-wage rules, youth wages, and employer coverage have their own details. Check current Nebraska Department of Labor guidance for exact requirements.

Overtime, part-time, and variable hours

Federal law generally requires overtime at 1.5× the regular rate for non-exempt employees working more than 40 hours in a workweek. Enter regular hours and overtime hours separately.

The overtime rate starts at 1.5× your wage until you type a different rate. Salary checks can add overtime the same way, as an extra hourly amount on top of annual pay.

Part-time workers often find federal withholding is small or zero, because annualized pay falls near the standard deduction.

FICA still applies from the first dollar, and Nebraska tax starts once wages after allowances pass the lowest bracket threshold.

If you work two hourly jobs, each employer withholds as if it were your only job. That can leave you under-withheld.

The federal W-4 multiple-jobs step and extra Nebraska withholding on the W-4N are the usual fixes. Nebraska tax withholding explains how allowances interact with the state tables.

Turn a rate into gross pay for any schedule

An hourly paycheck calculator for Nebraska starts with hours, not a salary box. Gross pay for the week is your rate times regular hours, plus overtime hours times the overtime rate.

The tool then treats that week as if it repeated all year, withholds tax on the annual picture, and divides the result back into the paycheck you actually receive.

The hours box on this page is always a week, even if you are paid every other week or twice a month. Forty hours at $20 is $800 that week and $41,600 if the week repeats all year. Pay frequency only decides how that year is sliced into checks: 52 weekly deposits, 26 biweekly ones, 24 semi-monthly ones, or 12 monthly ones.

A biweekly check at that schedule is $1,600 gross. A semi-monthly check is a bit larger, about $1,733, because the same $41,600 is divided by 24 instead of 26. Do not type 80 into the hours box just because the paycheck covers two weeks. That would double the year.

A worked overtime week

Federal overtime for most non-exempt jobs is 1.5 times the regular rate after 40 hours in the workweek. At $20, time-and-a-half is $30. Five overtime hours add $150 of gross pay, and the week becomes $950 instead of $800.

On a 2027 weekly check, single, one allowance, this calculator estimates about $72 of federal tax, about $73 of FICA, about $29 of Nebraska tax, and about $776 net. That is roughly $114 more net than the straight 40-hour week, not the full $150, because each extra dollar is taxed.

Enter regular hours and overtime hours in separate fields. The overtime rate starts at 1.5 times your wage, and you can type a different rate when a contract or a weighted-average week uses another figure.

The form has one wage and one overtime rate. It will not blend a day rate and a night differential for you. If the week mixed rates, type the overtime rate you were actually paid, or average the regular rate yourself before you enter it.

Part-time weeks and uneven schedules

Twenty hours at $18 is $360 of weekly gross, or $18,720 a year. The same 2027 assumptions produce a very small federal withholding line, about $7 of Nebraska tax, and FICA of about $28. Estimated net is about $320, close to 89% of gross.

Part-time hourly pay often looks lightly taxed because annualized wages sit near the federal standard deduction. FICA still starts at the first dollar. Nebraska withholding starts once wages after the W-4N allowance clear the bottom of the Circular EN table.

If your hours swing, run the slow week and the busy week instead of averaging them first. Tax brackets make the average of two net checks a poor stand-in for the net of an average check, especially once overtime appears in only one of the weeks.

A school-year schedule or a harvest season is the same idea stretched across the calendar. Annualize the weeks you actually work when you are asking what the paycheck will be, and annualize a full year only when you are asking what the W-2 will show.

Benefits, a second job, and the city on the stub

Health premiums taken through a Section 125 plan reduce the wages that federal tax, FICA, and Nebraska tax are figured on. A traditional 401(k) deferral reduces federal and Nebraska wages and leaves FICA wages alone. A Roth deferral and most union dues come out after tax.

Put the yearly dollar amount of those items in the benefits fields if you want the hourly net to reflect them. Leave them blank when you are comparing two job offers before you know the plan.

Two hourly jobs are withheld as two separate jobs. Each employer applies the W-4 and W-4N as if it were the only one, so the combined checks can be short of the tax on the combined income. Extra Nebraska withholding on the W-4N, and the federal multiple-jobs step, are the usual repairs. The Nebraska tax withholding shows how allowance counts change the Nebraska line.

Omaha, Lincoln, Bellevue, and Grand Island do not add a city wage tax. The same rate and the same hours produce the same withholding estimate anywhere in the state. Rent and sales tax differ by city, which is a budget question rather than a paycheck question.

Reported tips are wages for withholding. A federal deduction for tips or overtime, if one is available for the year you file, is claimed on the return. It does not automatically shrink the tax on this week's stub. Check current IRS instructions before you count on a smaller check.

How to use the hourly fields

Leave the pay type on Hourly. Enter the rate you are actually paid, then a typical week of regular hours. Add overtime hours only for the week you are modeling.

Set the pay frequency to the number of checks your employer writes, not the number of weeks you work. Pick a 2027 pay date when you want the 2027 Nebraska tables. Filing status, W-4N allowances, and any extra withholding sit on the Federal and State tabs.

The result panel shows gross, each tax, and net for one check, plus the share of gross each line represents. A donut chart is a picture of that same split. If a number looks off, the first places to look are the pay date, the frequency, and whether a benefit was entered as a yearly amount.

Salaried offers belong on the Nebraska paycheck calculator, which starts from annual pay and can still add an overtime amount on top. This page stays with people whose base pay is an hourly rate.

Some workers also run an hourly estimate through a payroll company's calculator or a national site such as SmartAsset and compare the lines. This tool is a separate educational estimator. It is not those products, and it does not log into an employer account.

Reading the estimate next to a stub

Match gross first. If the stub's hours or rate differ from what you typed, every tax line will differ too. Then match FICA. Social Security plus Medicare should be 7.65% of FICA wages until you pass the Social Security wage base.

Federal and Nebraska lines are allowed to differ by a few dollars from this percentage-method estimate. Employers may use wage-bracket tables, and they may round each tax to the cent on a slightly different step.

A gap of a whole tax bracket usually means a different filing status, a different allowance count, extra withholding, or pre-tax deductions you did not enter. It can also mean the stub is from a 2026 pay date and you modeled 2027, or the reverse.

Keep the result as a planning range. It is not a pay stub, a W-2, or permission to change your withholding. For the order in which deductions hit a check, the Nebraska take-home pay walks through gross versus net without asking you to start from an hourly rate.

Nebraska hourly paycheck FAQs

How much is $20 an hour after taxes in Nebraska?

At 40 hours a week, single, paid weekly with one allowance, this tool estimates about $662 per week, or roughly $34,400 a year.

How do I calculate a biweekly paycheck from an hourly wage?

Multiply rate by weekly hours by 52, then divide by 26 for gross. Switch frequency to Biweekly above to see net.

Are tips and overtime taxed in Nebraska paychecks?

Reported tips and overtime are wages for withholding purposes. Federal deduction rules for tips or overtime are claimed when you file, so they may not change per-check withholding.

Check current IRS guidance.

Does working in Omaha or Lincoln change my hourly take-home?

No. Nebraska has no local wage tax, so the same rate nets the same pay statewide.

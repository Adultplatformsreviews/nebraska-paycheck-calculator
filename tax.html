What this Nebraska withholding calculator shows

This page focuses on one line of your stub: Nebraska state income tax.

The calculator above still shows the full picture. The explanations below cover how Nebraska figures state withholding, what your W-4N allowances do, and why your state tax per check is what it is.

For general take-home planning, start at the main Nebraska paycheck calculator.

How this withholding estimate works

The calculator uses the Circular EN percentage method for the pay date's year: subtract pre-tax wages, subtract the W-4N allowance for that frequency, then apply that year's row. The worked steps, including the $60,000 biweekly example, are on Nebraska Circular EN.

How many allowances to claim, when to hand in a new form, and the per-period allowance amounts are on Nebraska W-4N. Change the allowance count above to see the Nebraska line move on your own wage.

Withholding is not your final Nebraska tax

What is withheld from paychecks is a prepayment. Your actual Nebraska income tax is settled on Form 1040N, which uses your full-year income, Nebraska's personal exemption credit, and any credits you claim.

A refund means too much was withheld, and a balance due means too little. If you have side income, consider extra W-4N withholding or estimated payments.

Bonuses and other supplemental wages

When a bonus, commission, or taxable award is paid in the same check as regular wages, Circular EN treats the total as one wage payment for that payroll period. The percentage method then runs on the combined amount.

When the supplemental pay is a separate payment, the employer may add it to regular wages for the current or last period and withhold on the total, or it may withhold a flat 3.5% on the supplemental wages. Overtime that is already in your hourly rate belongs in the hourly calculator. A one-time bonus is this supplemental rule. The dollar comparison is in Nebraska bonus tax.

A flat 3.5% withheld today is still an estimate. The income is taxed under the brackets when you file Form 1040N, and any gap becomes a refund or a balance due. Federal supplemental withholding, often 22% on bonuses under the IRS aggregate or flat methods, is a separate federal step and is not the Nebraska 3.5%.

What the withholding formula can see

Pre-tax deductions that the booklet treats as excluded from taxable wages come off before the allowance subtraction. In this calculator, traditional retirement deferrals and Section 125 benefits both reduce the Nebraska wage. Post-tax deductions do not.

Extra withholding on the W-4N is a flat dollar amount added after the table. It does not change which row you land in. People with a second job, a spouse's income, or self-employment profit often use that line because allowances alone cannot see the other income.

The federal W-4 does not set the Nebraska allowance count. Publication 15-T still drives federal income tax in this estimator, using the 2026 percentage-method worksheets until the IRS publishes the next set. Nebraska tax on a 2027 pay date does not wait for that federal update.

A nonresident who works both in Nebraska and in other states can give the employer Form 9N and name the percentage of wages subject to Nebraska withholding. The employer figures tax on the full wage, then multiplies by that percentage. Someone working in Nebraska all the time does not use Form 9N to wipe the tax off. A nonresident servicemember's spouse who elects the servicemember's other state of residence uses Form 9N so Nebraska tax is not withheld.

That percentage is only for withholding. It does not set the Nebraska wage box on the W-2. For tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2025, Circular EN also describes narrow cases where withholding is not required on some nonresident pay, including seven or fewer duty days in the state and certain convenience-rule wages. This calculator does not apply those exceptions. It treats the wages you enter as fully subject to Nebraska tax.

Withholding compared with Form 1040N

Withholding is a prepayment. Form 1040N computes the year's Nebraska tax from federal adjusted gross income, Nebraska adjustments, and the credits the return allows. Social Security benefits are exempt from Nebraska income tax. A slice of military retirement pay can be excluded. Neither of those adjustments is something a typical wage table can apply to a weekly check.

On the $60,000 example, one allowance, single, 2027, the state line is $73.80 a check, or about $1,919 across 26 checks. That is about 3.2% of gross wages withheld. It is not automatically 3.2% of Nebraska taxable income, because the return starts from a different income figure and applies credits the paycheck never saw.

A refund means the prepayments, including withholding, exceeded the tax on the return. A balance due means they fell short. Changing allowances changes the prepayment. It does not change the tax on the same year's income unless your life actually changes. The longer comparison, including W-2 boxes, is in Nebraska Form 1040N vs paycheck withholding.

The 2027 filing rates, 2.46%, 3.51%, and a top rate of 3.99%, live on the return. The 2026 top filing rate is 4.55%. The withholding rows for a 2027 pay date step from about 2.26% to about 4.03%. Both can be true, because they are different schedules. The Nebraska income tax brackets is the place for the filing rates. This page stays with the amount the employer sends in from the check.

How to read the withholding result

Use the calculator above when the question is "how much Nebraska tax comes out of this check?" Set the pay date, the frequency, the filing status the employer will use, and the allowance count. Add extra withholding if the W-4N has a dollar amount on that line.

The Nebraska line in the result is the estimate. Federal tax and FICA are shown so you can see the state line in context, and the full take-home picture lives on the Nebraska paycheck calculator.

If you are deciding how many allowances to claim, or whether you must file a new W-4N by February 15 to renew an exemption, use the Nebraska W-4N. The percentage-method math is on Nebraska Circular EN.

Compare a real stub with the same gross and the same allowances before you treat a difference as an error. Bracket-table employers, the 1.5% special rule, and rounding all create small gaps that are still "right" under Circular EN. What the booklet is, step by step, is Nebraska Circular EN.

Nebraska income tax calculator FAQs

How much Nebraska state tax comes out of my paycheck?

For a $60,000 single filer with one allowance paid biweekly, about $82 per check in 2026 and about $74 in 2027, roughly 3% to 3.5% of gross. Use the calculator above for your numbers.

How many W-4N allowances should I claim?

Most single filers with one job claim one, and households with dependents claim more. If you have multiple jobs or side income, claim fewer or add extra withholding.

Compare your last 1040N result to decide.

Is Nebraska income tax flat or progressive?

Progressive. A 2026 pay date uses withholding rates from about 2.26% to 4.60%.

A 2027 pay date uses about 2.26% to 4.03%.

Does Nebraska tax Social Security or retirement payroll deductions?

Traditional 401(k) deferrals are excluded from Nebraska withholding wages. Nebraska has fully exempted Social Security benefits from state income tax in recent years, but that applies to benefits, not the FICA you pay from wages. Does Nebraska tax Social Security is the benefit question.
